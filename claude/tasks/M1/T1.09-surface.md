## Deliverable

```rust
/// Every point a player could stand on, sampled every SURFACE_SAMPLE_STEP columns.
pub fn extract_surface(mask: &Mask) -> Vec<Point>;

/// Is this a valid standing position? Used here, and again at runtime by
/// respawn (T4.13) and periodic item spawns (T4.05) after the map is damaged.
pub fn is_standable(mask: &Mask, x: i32, y: i32) -> bool;
```

## Deliverable detail

`is_standable(x, y)` is true when **all** of:
- `(x, y)` is air;
- `(x, y+1)` is solid;
- the `PLAYER_W × PLAYER_H` box whose bottom edge is at `y` and whose centre column
  is `x` is entirely air;
- there are at least `HEAD_CLEARANCE` px of air directly above `y`.

`extract_surface` scans columns at `SURFACE_SAMPLE_STEP` (16) spacing and, within
each column, collects **every** y that is standable — not just the topmost.

## Notes

Collecting every standable y per column, rather than only the highest, is
deliberate and important: it finds cave floors, ledges under overhangs, and island
tops. Players walk on all of those, and validation (T1.10), spawns (T1.12) and item
placement (T4.04) all consume this set. Taking only the outer skin would make
half the map invisible to the rest of the pipeline.

`is_standable` is exported and reused at runtime because the map gets destroyed.
`MapMeta.surface_points` is a snapshot of the pristine map; by minute three many of
those points are mid-air. Every runtime consumer re-checks with this function. Do
not let a second copy of this logic appear anywhere else.

Keep it cheap — it is called per-pixel here and per-spawn at runtime. The head
clearance check can early-out on the first solid pixel found.

## Tests

Build small masks by hand:

- Flat floor across the map: one surface point per sampled column, all at the same
  y, and the count equals `w / SURFACE_SAMPLE_STEP`.
- A one-pixel-wide spike: not standable (the player box does not fit).
- A ceiling `HEAD_CLEARANCE − 1` px above the floor: not standable.
- A ceiling `HEAD_CLEARANCE + 1` px above: standable.
- A two-level cave (floor, roof, then another floor above): the column yields
  **two** surface points.
- A solid column with no air above: no surface points.
- An all-air mask: no surface points.
- Points are ordered deterministically (by x, then y) — later passes rely on it for
  reproducibility.
- `is_standable` returns false one pixel to either side of a narrow ledge that is
  exactly `PLAYER_W` wide, and true at its centre.
- On a real generated map (medium scale), the surface point count is in a plausible
  band (roughly 200–4000) — a count near zero means the earlier passes broke.
