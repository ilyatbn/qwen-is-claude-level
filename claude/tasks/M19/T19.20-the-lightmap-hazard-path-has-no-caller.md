# T19.20 — The lightmap's light-source path has no caller (found by T19.13)

Milestone: M19 · Depends on: nothing · Size: unknown until the 14 tests are read

## What was seen

`collectLightSources` (`client/src/render/lightmap-math.ts:119`) has **no production
caller**. Grepped across `client/` and `scripts/`:

```
     14 client/src/render/lightmap-math.test.ts
      2 client/src/render/lightmap-math.ts     (the definition, and the doc-comment
                                                above HAZARD_RADIUS that says so)
```

Zero call sites outside its own tests. `lightmap-math.ts:98` already records the fact in
prose — T19.13 found it while refusing to add a `flame` row to `HAZARD_RADIUS`, because
the row would have lit nothing.

What the live path does instead: `GameScene.ts:1498` builds its `LightSource[]` from three
things and nothing else — the local player's `fov` as a radial, `OrdnanceState.lights()`
and `OrdnanceFxState.lights()`.

## Why it is more than tidying

**It is the twelve-mechanisms shape with a test suite on top**, which is worse than none:
16 assertions make it read as maintained code. But the exposure is not the function, it is
the four light sources it is the only producer of, none of which the live list carries:

- **Flashlight cones — local and remote.** `collectLightSources` is the **only** producer
  of `kind: 'cone'` anywhere in the client (grep `"'cone'"`: two sites, both inside it).
  `lightmap.ts:145`'s `eraseCone` branch and `eraseCone` itself therefore have no live
  producer either. `docs/14` §4 makes a flashlight at night a **trade** — long sightlines
  bought at the cost of being seen first — and the function's own doc-comment names
  remote cones as "the one that is easy to omit and matters most".
- **Explosion flashes** (`FLASH_DECAY`, radius `220 * t`).
- **Hazard lights** — `HAZARD_RADIUS`'s lava / burn / meteor.

Whether any of those *is* visible today is exactly the question this task must answer with
pixels, not by reading: §C2 and `docs/72`. A flashlight that lights nothing at night looks
identical in the simulation to one that works.

## What was ruled out

- **Not a naming miss.** The grep above is over both `client/` and `scripts/`, and the
  only non-test references are in the defining file.
- **Not a recent regression.** T19.13 read the same state and wrote it into
  `lightmap-math.ts:98` rather than acting on it, deliberately, because §F10.3's flame row
  was the task in hand.

## Where to start

**Read what the 14 tests actually assert, and say so in the journal, before either
repair.** Anything they alone cover is the real exposure and is unprotected today — the
same instruction T19.19 carries, for the same reason.

Then decide, and the two options are not equivalent:

- **Wire it to `GameScene`.** The live list would become `collectLightSources(...)`, which
  means feeding it `WorldLights` — remote players with `aim` and `flashlightOn`, the
  explosion list with ages, the hazard list. Check each of those is available at that
  point in `update()` before promising it. Anything that lights up as a result must be
  proved with a screenshot and a control frame (a night frame with the flashlight off),
  not with a counter: §A15, and `chutesDrawn` read 1 on a build with every parachute draw
  call deleted.
- **Delete it with its tests**, and delete `eraseCone` with it if the cone stays
  unproduced. That is the honest outcome *only* if the four sources above are judged not
  to belong in the game — which is a design call `docs/14` §4 appears to have already
  made the other way for the flashlight, so a deletion needs that contradiction named
  rather than skipped.

Do not do both halves blind, and do not add a `flame` row on the way past: T19.13 already
established that a flame lights the world through `GLOW` and `OrdnanceState.lights()`.

## Tests

- Whatever lands, **falsify it at the live binding site**. If it is wired: turn the
  flashlight off in the state handed to the lightmap and show the frame goes dark where it

  coverage went with it.
- **A control region and a control frame** for any pixel claim (`docs/72` §C2). "Bright
  where the cone points" is satisfied by a frame that is bright everywhere.

## Resolved 2026-09-08 — deleted, and it overrides live spec

**The human ruled: a flashlight is a pure upgrade.** Their words, relayed through the
coordinator on 2026-09-08: *"leave it as is. this is as intented."*

**One provenance note, because it nearly cost this deletion its footing.** An earlier
report of mine attributed a second, longer quote to the human — that flashlights "have no
downside" and "other players dont see anything different". **That text has no source I can
point to.** It was my own inference from their asking to keep things simple, written as if
it were something they had said, and the coordinator was right to refuse to act on it and
to hold this task until a ruling arrived that could be checked. The conclusion happened to
match; the citation was still manufactured, and a right answer reached through an invented
quote is not evidence of anything. The single sentence above is the whole of the record.

So option two of the two above: **deleted, with its tests.**

**The spec is amended: `docs/76` §G2** withdraws both of `docs/14` §4's affected bullets —
the `F` toggle (gone since T20.07) and the beacon clause. So this deletion follows the
amended spec rather than overriding a live one. No builder touched `docs/`.

**§G2 was corrected after this landed** (`d1e0c56`), and the two claims I reported are now
right in the doc: there is **no cone on screen at all**, not even for the carrier, and the
ambient radius is **widened** by `FLASHLIGHT_FOV_MULT` 1.5 rather than reduced. §G2 now states
the shipped item in one line — *a wider view, no cone, no toggle, no tell, and no fuel cost.*

Left standing by §G2's own instruction: `FLASHLIGHT_RANGE` (260) and `FLASHLIGHT_CONE_DEG`
(55) are defined, bridged through wasm, typed in TS, and **read by nothing** after this
deletion. Retiring them is a separate change and should be booked, not folded in here.

### What went

- `lightmap-math.ts` — `collectLightSources`, `LightSourceSpec`, `PlayerLight`,
  `WorldLights`, `FLASH_DECAY`, `HAZARD_RADIUS`.
- `lightmap.ts` — `eraseCone`, the `kind === 'cone'` branch, and `LightSource`'s
  `kind` / `angle` / `coneDeg`. With cones gone there is one kind of light, and a
  one-variant discriminant advertises a second kind that does not exist.
- `lightmap-math.test.ts` — the nine `collectLightSources` tests.
- `GameScene` (3) and `SandboxScene` (4) construction sites lost their `kind: 'radial'`.

### What stopped being guarded — named, not counted

Daylight no-op; the local radial; the local cone; the ambient-radius widening; a remote
player's cone and its no-flashlight control; explosion-flash decay over `FLASH_DECAY`;
lava / burn / meteor hazard lights; fog shrinking every radius.

**Only the widening had another home** — `fovRadius`, still tested, including against the
Rust copy. The other eight guarded a path nothing ever ran, which is why deleting them
costs no coverage of anything the game does.

T20.07's implementer left a note on `PlayerLight.hasFlashlight` asking that a
permanently-on cone be confirmed before it reached a screen. It was asked; the answer was
no. The note went with the code it guarded rather than lingering pointing at nothing.

### Proof that nothing dangles

`tsc --noEmit` clean, and every surviving mention of the seven removed symbols is prose in
the two deletion notes. The `'cone'` hits in `GameScene.ts` and `scripts/checks/ordnance.mjs`
are the **melee swing cone** the server narrates — a different mechanism that shares a word.
