# T19.23 — `bullets-visible`'s window is the screenshot itself (v7)

Milestone: M19 · Depends on: nothing · Size: unknown — measure before designing

Split out of the retired suite-context hypothesis. **Do not approach this like T19.22.**

## What was seen
`bullets-visible` goes red inside a full suite and green standalone — but unlike
`night-combat` it has **essentially no waits to fix**: 1 bare wait, 0 condition polls.

> **CENSUS RE-VALIDATED 2026-09-06 and it still holds** — exactly one bare wait, `sleep(160)`.
> **Count the right idiom.** This check does not call `waitForTimeout` at all; it uses the
> harness's `sleep` and `standStill` (four calls). A `grep -c waitForTimeout` here returns
> **0** and invites the conclusion that the file was rewritten and the booking is stale.
> Neither is true. This is the claim-reported-through-something-else shape, caught while
> re-validating this very booking: the task says *bare wait*, and `waitForTimeout` is not
> what that means in this file. Its
timing dependence is **inherent to the measurement**: a screenshot costs ~150 ms and a round
crosses the screen in ~475 ms, so it fires 6 shots and needs >=2 catches. Under load the
screenshot cadence slows and the catches thin out.

## What is ruled out
- **"Audit the instrument" is not the answer here.** It has *already* had the audit
  `hud-timer` received in T19.15: brightest column rather than a mean, every reading
  differenced against a control frame, and a control region the round provably never enters
  (added after a streak once travelled 219 px across an earlier "control" that was not one).
  It is hardened and still timing-sensitive.
- **Replacing sleeps cannot work** — there are none to replace.

> **PREMISE OVERTURNED BY MEASUREMENT 2026-09-08. It fails when sampling is FASTER, not slower.**
>
> | condition | sample interval | bright samples | verdict |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | under load (8 hogs) ×4 | 254–300 ms | 2 | **pass** |
> | idle ×3 | 146–163 ms | 3 | **pass** |
> | the failing gate run | **143 ms** | **4** | **FAIL** |
>
> Thinning catches is benign — two monotone samples satisfy the rule. What breaks it is
> catching a **fourth**: `688 → 546 → 342 → 342`. The round flew and **arrived**; the extra
> sample read its resting place, and one zero step rejects the sequence.
>
> **So three of the four candidates below would make it worse** — cheaper capture, longer
> region and more shots all yield *more* samples. Measured rather than reasoned: PNG is
> **161 ms** (119 shot + 42 decode) against **JPEG-80 260 ms** and **JPEG-50 267 ms**, so the
> cheap-capture idea is also dead on its own terms; the cost is the 1045×620 capture itself.
> An in-page pixel read would be the real 10× win and needs `preserveDrawingBuffer: true` — a
> production render cost on every player's frame, to serve a test. **Refused**, on the same
> grounds as a server time-of-day override for `lava-lights`.
>
> **The rule is unsatisfiable by construction once sampling outlives the flight.** "Every
> consecutive step forward, including the last" cannot hold if a sample lands after arrival,
> and whether one does *is* the timing dependence. **It has been passing by the luck of
> sampling stopping in time.**
>
> Two fixes were built and rejected, each killed by its own check before a browser ran:
> **early exit once `travelled()` is satisfied** turns `STALL after 4th update` from FAIL to
> **PASS** — it retires the regression the check exists for. **Filtering on `live > 0`** is
> refuted by the trace: after the round stops being bright at `t=451`, `live` stays **1** until
> `t=927`, so it does not mark the end of flight.

## RULING 2026-09-08 — change the movement rule, because the change is strictly stronger

`HANDOFF-M19` fences this off: *lengthen the sampled region, never loosen the movement rule.*
**That fence forbids weakening, and this is not a weakening.** Today's rule accepts `dx > 40`
regardless of elapsed time. Tying the forward run to the shipped physics — requiring observed
travel to be a stated fraction of `SMG_MUZZLE_SPEED × dt × pxPerWorld`, which the check already
computes — is a **stronger** claim, and it permits stationary samples only after that bound is
met. The fence's intent is honoured; its letter changes.

**A rule that passes by luck is not a rule**, and `CLAUDE.md` is explicit that a gate failing on
a coin flip gates nothing: *remove such an assertion or fix its cause; do not weaken it.* This
fixes the cause.

**The bar, and it is not negotiable:** replay the recorded sequences through the new rule and
show that **`STALL after 4th update` still FAILS**. One "safe" fix already silently retired that
regression, so a fix that has not been replayed against it is not evidence. Paste both
sequences and both verdicts.

> **THE RULING ABOVE PRESCRIBED THE WRONG FIX, AND MEASUREMENT KILLED IT. Corrected
> 2026-09-08, after it was built and falsified rather than argued about.**
>
> I asked for a distance bound tied to `SMG_MUZZLE_SPEED`, justified by "423 px arrived against
> 69 px frozen". **The 69 px was never a measurement.** A real frozen build covers **251 px**,
> because `projectile_move` arrives at the *network* rate, not `SIM_HZ`. Against a live round's
> 274–424 px that is **1.09× apart**, so **no distance floor can separate the two populations**
> — any floor that fails a frozen build also fails real ones. The builder implemented my bound,
> froze `projectile_move` after the 4th update at the live binding site, and **the check still
> passed, twice.**
>
> The same 69 px claim was also in the shipped comment at `bullets-visible.mjs::travelled`, so
> it had been sitting in the code unchallenged. Both are wrong; both are removed.
>
> **What does separate them, measured across 28 runs, is the length of the stationary tail:**
>
> | | trailing stationary samples |
> |---|---|
> | 26 consecutive live runs | **0**, every one |
> | the recorded gate failure | **1** |
> | frozen build | **3 and 4** |
>
> A round that lands **despawns**, so it cannot be drawn stationary sample after sample. So the
> rule allows **exactly one** trailing stationary sample — the arrival, which is the flake —
> and keeps every-step-forward otherwise. That is a claim about the shipped despawn behaviour,
> not a threshold fitted to today's numbers.
>
> **Evidence that it meets the bar**: replay through the shipped function's *own source text*
> (extracted from the file, not a replica) gives 14/14 correct verdicts including
> `STALL after 4th update` FAIL and both measured frozen sequences FAIL; browser falsification
> at the live binding site is 2/2 FAIL frozen and 6/6 green reverted.
>
> **One failure remains unattributed**, and is recorded as unexplained rather than explained
> away: during development a run failed while the rejected 139 px floor was in place, and its
> output had been piped through `grep`, so the failing assertion is unknown. 26 subsequent runs
> were green and none came near a distance floor.



## The constraint that must not be violated
`HANDOFF-M19.md` is explicit: **lengthen the sampled region, never loosen the movement
rule.** The movement rule is the only assertion a hitscan build cannot pass; weakening it
retires the thing the check exists for.

## Deliverable
Measure first: what is the achieved sample interval per shot, idle versus inside a full
suite? Only then design. Candidates, none pre-chosen — a cheaper capture than a full
screenshot, a longer sampled region, more shots, or a capture that does not compete with the
suite. **Report the measurement even if no fix follows from it.**
