## Deliverable

```ts
export class RemoteInterpolator {
  constructor(bufferMs?: number);   // default INTERP_DELAY_MS

  push(tick: number, serverTime: number, players: readonly SnapshotPlayer[]): void;

  /** State to render right now, for every known remote player. */
  sample(now: number): Map<number, InterpolatedPlayer>;

  readonly stats: {
    bufferDepth: number; extrapolatingMs: number; frozen: boolean;
  };
}

/** Simple EWMA offset estimator with outlier rejection. */
export class ClockSync {
  addSample(serverTime: number, localTime: number, rtt: number): void;
  get offset(): number;
  get rtt(): number;
}
```

Sampling:
```
renderTime = now - offset - INTERP_DELAY_MS
find snapshots a, b bracketing renderTime
t = (renderTime - a.time) / (b.time - a.time)
pos = lerp(a.pos, b.pos, t)
aim = shortestArcLerp(a.aim, b.aim, t)
```

## Notes

**100 ms is two snapshot intervals at 20 Hz**, so one dropped snapshot still leaves
a bracketing pair and interpolation continues without a hitch. That is why the
number is what it is — do not reduce it to "feel more responsive"; remote players
are not responsive, they are accurate.

**Aim must interpolate the short way around the circle.** Naive lerp of the raw
`u16` makes a player's weapon spin a full turn whenever the angle crosses the wrap
point (`docs/22-aiming-crosshair.md` §5). Use `wrapToPi` on the delta.

**Extrapolation is capped at 250 ms.** With no future snapshot, extrapolate using
the snapshot's velocity, then **freeze**. Extrapolating longer produces players
sliding confidently through walls, which is worse than a momentarily stationary
figure. Report `frozen` in the stats so T8.03 can show it.

Remote players are **never** run through `applyInput` — there are no remote inputs
to run. Interpolating transmitted positions is both cheaper and more accurate.

The buffer holds ~1 s of snapshots (20 entries) and drops the oldest. Do not let it
grow.

`ClockSync` is deliberately simple: an EWMA of the offset with 3σ outlier rejection,
using socket.io's own ping/pong for RTT. Nothing gameplay-critical depends on it —
only event ordering and the interpolation clock — which is why it does not need to
be better than this.

A player who leaves is removed from the buffer's tracked set; a player who joins
mid-stream starts interpolating once two snapshots exist and is rendered at the
snapshot position until then.

## Tests

`client/src/net/interpolation.test.ts` — pure logic, no Phaser:

- With two snapshots 50 ms apart, sampling at the midpoint gives the midpoint
  position.
- Sampling picks the correct bracketing pair from a buffer of 20.
- **Out-of-order snapshot arrival** still yields correct bracketing (insert 3, then
  2, then sample between them).
- A missing middle snapshot still interpolates smoothly across the gap.
- With no future snapshot, extrapolation uses velocity and the position advances.
- Extrapolation stops after exactly 250 ms and `frozen` becomes true.
- Once a new snapshot arrives, `frozen` clears and interpolation resumes without a
  jump.
- **Aim takes the short arc**: interpolating from 350° to 10° passes through 0°, not
  through 180°.
- Aim interpolation from 10° to 350° goes backwards through 0°.
- A player removed from snapshots is dropped from `sample` output.
- A newly appearing player is rendered at their snapshot position until two exist.
- The buffer is capped and drops the oldest entries.
- `ClockSync` converges to a constant offset within 20 samples.
- `ClockSync` rejects a sample 10σ out and its offset barely moves.
