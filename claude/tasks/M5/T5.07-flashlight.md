## Notes

The flashlight is an **inventory item, not a default ability** — the brief is
explicit. Toggled with `F` or by selecting it. The toggle is server-mirrored
(T4.13's `use_item` already handles the no-consume case), so everyone sees your cone.

**Your cone is visible to other players.** A flashlight at night is a beacon: long
sightlines at the cost of being seen first. Include remote players' cones in
`collectLightSources` — omitting them is the easy mistake, and it silently removes
the entire trade-off that makes the item interesting.

The flashlight **reduces** your ambient FoV to `FLASHLIGHT_AMBIENT_MULT` (0.65)
while giving the cone. It is a trade, not a pure upgrade. T5.06's `fov_radius`
already encodes this; make sure the client uses that value and does not compute its
own.

Dynamic lights to include: explosions (bright, ~0.2 s decay), lava jets, burning
ground, meteors in flight, item glints. Shooting in the dark should give away your
position — that is intended.

**Verify the TypeScript `fovRadius` from T3.10 against the Rust `fov_radius` from
T5.06.** Either call through WASM (preferred) or add a test that compares the two
across a grid of inputs. Two independent copies of this formula is exactly the drift
this project's architecture exists to prevent; if you leave the TS copy, say so
explicitly in your report.

Pre-render the cone texture once at boot and rotate it per source. Building a cone
path per light per frame is the performance trap here, the same as the radial
gradient in T3.10.

## Tests

`client/src/render/lightmap.test.ts`:

- `collectLightSources` in full daylight returns an empty list (no lightmap work).
- At night with the local player only, it returns one radial source at the player.
- With the flashlight on, it returns the radial source **plus** a cone at the aim
  angle.
- The cone's angle matches the player's aim and its spread is
  `FLASHLIGHT_CONE_DEG`.
- **A remote player with a flashlight contributes a cone** — this is the visibility
  trade-off test.
- A remote player without one contributes nothing.
- An explosion contributes a bright source that decays to nothing over ~0.2 s.
- Lava and burning ground contribute sources while active.
- The local player's ambient radius is `FLASHLIGHT_AMBIENT_MULT` smaller when the
  light is on.
- Fog reduces every radial source's radius by `fogMult`.
- **Cross-check**: the TS FoV value matches the Rust `fov_radius` for a grid of
  (darkness, fog, health, flashlight) combinations.
