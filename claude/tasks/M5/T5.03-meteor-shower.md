## Deliverable

```rust
pub struct MeteorShower { rng: ChaCha8Rng, next_spawn_at: f32 }

impl MeteorShower {
    pub fn new(seed: u64, now: f32) -> Self;

    /// Spawn meteors on cadence into the shared projectile pool.
    pub fn tick(&mut self, projectiles: &mut Projectiles, map: &Map,
                active: bool, now: f32) -> Vec<ProjectileId>;

    /// Handle a meteor impact: carve, damage, spawn fragments.
    pub fn on_impact(projectiles: &mut Projectiles, map: &mut Map,
                     players: &mut [PlayerHitTarget], at: Vec2,
                     is_fragment: bool, seed: u64, now: f32) -> ExplosionResult;
}
```

Behaviour:
- While active, every `METEOR_EVERY` (0.5 s), spawn a meteor at a random x,
  `y = -32`, with `METEOR_SPEED` (700) downward plus a small lateral velocity.
- Meteors are **ordinary projectiles** — they fall under gravity and step against
  the mask through T4.09.
- On impact: carve `METEOR_CARVE_R` (50), deal `METEOR_DAMAGE` (55) with falloff,
  and spawn `METEOR_FRAGMENTS` (6) fragments at `METEOR_FRAG_SPEED` (320–520)
  spread across the **upward** hemisphere.
- A fragment impact carves `METEOR_FRAG_CARVE_R` (14) and deals
  `METEOR_FRAG_DAMAGE` (18).
- **Fragments do not spawn fragments.**

## Notes

Reusing `Projectiles` (T4.09) rather than writing a bespoke meteor simulation is the
right call: meteors already need gravity, sub-stepped terrain collision and player
AABB tests, and duplicating that is how the two paths drift apart.

Add a `MeteorFragment` weapon id to the weapon table, or carry an explicit flag on
the projectile. Either works; say which you chose. The important property is that
`is_fragment` is knowable at impact so recursion is prevented.

**Fragments must not recurse.** Six fragments each spawning six is 36, then 216 —
the tick stops terminating in about four generations. The `is_fragment` guard is the
whole safety mechanism; test it directly.

Fragments spread across the upward hemisphere (angles in `−π..0` in screen
coordinates), not a full circle — fragments driven straight into the ground they
just came from are wasted.

This is the effect that reshapes the map most, which is its purpose. Over 10 seconds
it produces 20 impacts and 120 fragments, carving roughly 20·π·50² + 120·π·14² ≈
230 000 pixels — a visible fraction of a medium map.

Use `explode` (T4.10) for both impacts and fragments, so shields, i-frames,
knockback and buried reveals all work without re-implementation.

## Tests

- Over `METEOR_DURATION` (10 s), exactly `10 / 0.5` = 20 meteors spawn.
- No meteors while `active` is false.
- Meteors spawn above the map and fall downward.
- A meteor impact carves `METEOR_CARVE_R` — `count_solid` drops by roughly `π·50²`.
- A meteor impact deals `METEOR_DAMAGE` at the epicentre with linear falloff.
- A meteor impact spawns exactly `METEOR_FRAGMENTS` fragments.
- **Fragments spawn zero fragments** — simulate a full shower to completion and
  assert the total projectile count stays bounded. This is the recursion test and
  it is the most important one here.
- Fragment velocities are within `METEOR_FRAG_SPEED` and directed upward.
- A fragment impact carves `METEOR_FRAG_CARVE_R` and deals `METEOR_FRAG_DAMAGE`.
- A shielded player takes half from both.
- A meteor impact overlapping a buried slot reveals it.
- A full shower on a medium map completes within a bounded number of ticks and does
  not exceed a reasonable projectile count (assert a ceiling like 200).
- Determinism: the same seed produces the same impact positions and times.
