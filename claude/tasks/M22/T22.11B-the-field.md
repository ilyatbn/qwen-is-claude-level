# T22.11B — The field

Milestone: M22 · Depends on: **T22.11A** (`876cb9d`, the seam) and **T22.05A** (asteroids and
their levels) · Size: large · Origin: the split of `T22.11` (`R45`)

`T22.11A` built the seam and left `accel` as `Vec2::ZERO` at all three construction sites. This
task puts the field in it: every asteroid pulls, the pulls sum, and the sum is deterministic on
both sides. **This is where the mode stops being weightless and starts being many local wells.**

## The falloff is linear to a cutoff (`R47`) — and this is not a preference

    a(d) = a_surf(n) * max(0, 1 - d / R(n))

Inverse-square was costed and rejected with arithmetic that is in `R47`: pinned to an escapable
ceiling it delivers **4.4 px/s² at one climb budget** against an 1100 px/s² sideways thruster.
Brutal on contact or imperceptible at range, never both.

Linear-to-cutoff buys three things at once, and they are the reason:
1. `a_surf(n)` **is** the quantity R46's inequality bounds — the tunable and its guard are the
   same number, so the table cannot drift away from its own assertion.
2. `R(n) <= JETPACK_CLIMB_BUDGET` (= 780 px, and that constant already carries its basis) makes
   the reach sayable in one sentence a player can feel: *a full tank always clears the well.*
3. The acceleration reaches zero **continuously** at `R(n)`, which answers the design record's
   own objection that *"a well with a hard edge is a wall you fall off."*

## The escape ceiling is against `JETPACK_THRUST_DOWN` (`R46`)

**Do not write this guard against `JETPACK_THRUST_UP`.** R18 and the design record both did, and
R46 overturns them. The pack is anisotropic — up 2200, sideways 1100, **down 900** — so the
binding case is a player resting on the **underside** of a rock, who must push *downward* to
leave. A well of 1500 px/s² passes the guard as originally written and still traps that player
forever, with no cause on screen.

With `d_min(r) = SPACE_ASTEROID_CORE_FRAC * r + PLAYER_H / 2.0`:

    max over n <= SPACE_LEVEL_MAX, r <= SPACE_ASTEROID_R_MAX  of  a(n, r, d_min(r))
        <  JETPACK_THRUST_DOWN

Scope it to asteroids, so `T22.12`'s black hole can carry the inverse assertion without the two
tasks contradicting each other. Note the binding rock is **not** the biggest one: level 5 starts
at `r = 54`, and `d_min(54) = 54.5` is smaller than `d_min(64) = 62`, so the smallest level-5
rock is the tightest case. Assert it as a test over the whole table, not at one radius.

## **You may not borrow `no_tunnelling_…` as your speed guard** (`R50`)

Four documents, including two of my rulings, asserted that
`resolve::no_tunnelling_at_ten_times_terminal_velocity_through_integrate` would catch a
`max_speed` fault. `T22.11A`'s builder measured it: **it does not.** `Forces::gravity` carrying
`Some(MAX_FALL_SPEED)` leaves 1062/1062 passing, because `apply_gravity` has already clamped
`vel.y` to 900 and the magnitude clamp is an exact no-op on that path. The test also asserts a
**distance**, and an **upper** bound, which a speed clamp can only help.

**Write your own.** It must drive a body on the **vector** path — where no `vel.y` clamp has run —
past this mode's terminal speed, and assert a bound the clamp is the only thing that could
satisfy. `Forces::max_speed`'s doc comment records the measurement; read it before you write the
test, and add your test's name to it when it exists.

## Determinism, which is the hard half

- **Float summation is not associative.** Client and server must add the same contributions in
  the same order. Iterate a sorted, stable list; never a `HashMap`, for the reason
  `World::players` already gives at the code.
- **R36 is yours, and it is a red-before-green.** `World::state_hash` hashes the mask and
  **nothing** from `MapMeta` — confirmed by the scout at the symbol. The levels live in
  `MapMeta`. `level` *is* in the golden meta digest, so *generation* drift is caught; a level
  differing between server and client at *runtime* is invisible today. **Make a level differ
  between the two sides and show the state hash notices.** That test is the deliverable, not the
  hash line.
- **The client has no rocks at all** (`R49`, sharper than R11 said): `GameCore::new()` generates
  on the standard generator, so `meta.asteroids` is **empty**, not stale. The setter is
  `T22.11C`'s. **Say at the code what this task assumes about the client**, and do not quietly
  depend on a field that is not installed yet.

## Cost, and what the cutoff is actually for

N is **64 at most** (Large's target; Small 14, Medium 34, and these are rejection-sampling
targets, so realised counts are at or below them). Worst case 64 × 6 players = **384 pairs per
tick**. **The cutoff is not needed for performance at that N** — the design record's premise of
*"tons of tiny islands, so N is large"* does not survive the numbers. It is a **gameplay and
float-order** decision: it bounds how many terms enter each sum. Argue it on those grounds at
the code, and if you claim a cost, measure it rather than reasoning about it.

## Tests

- **Near accelerates, far does not** — and the fixture must have **rocks**. The scout's warning:
  all 20 tests in `player/space.rs` run on an asteroid-free fixture, so **not one of them can
  report a field that never fires**. `T22.11A` confirmed it by deleting `accel`'s application
  entirely and watching 1062/1062 still pass. Your first test closes that hole or nothing does.
- **Escape is possible from every level on a full tank**, asserted at the ceiling, **downward**.
- Level 5 is measurably harder to escape than level 1 — a ratio, against the stated basis.
- Two asteroids pull a player between them and the result is the sum, in a fixed order.
- **R36's cross-side test**, red first.
- **Standard and low gravity are untouched** — the control that says this did not change the
  game everyone else is playing. The golden table must not move.

## Hazards

- **R14: players only.** Projectiles, items, crates, tombstones and mines are not attracted;
  `T22.11A` already put every non-player body at scale 0 in space through `Forces::falling`.
  One mechanism note, because the design record got it wrong once: bullets are unaffected
  because `projectile.rs::integrate` early-returns on `Delivery::Bullet` **before** it reads
  `gravity_scale`, not because of a zero in the weapon table.
- **Orientation is ruled: players stay upright** (the design record's open question, answered
  `no`). Do not rotate anything.
- **The box must be yours** for anything timed; `pgrep` yourself first.
