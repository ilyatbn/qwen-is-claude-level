# T22.12 — The black hole

Milestone: M22 · Depends on: **T22.05A** (asteroids to eat) and **T22.11** (the attractor it
shares) · Size: **large** · Origin: new, 2026-09-18

Asked for: *"add a new hazard. black hole. this is a permanent one and appears randomly at the
last minute. it will destroy one of the many random 'asteroids' and suck players into it if
they get close. they die immmediately. if you get within the range of it, you cannot escape."*

## What it is for, so it is not tuned like the others

Every other hazard in M22 is an obstacle. **This one ends the round.** It arrives in the last
minute, it never leaves, and it takes an asteroid with it — so it shrinks the arena and pushes
the remaining players together at exactly the point where a round would otherwise be two people
hiding. It is the closing pressure, and it should be tuned as that rather than as a fourth
thing to dodge.

## It is the deliberate exception to `T22.11`'s escape guarantee

`T22.11` asserts that **escape is possible from every asteroid level on a full tank**, because a
well nobody can leave kills with no cause on screen. The owner has now asked for exactly that
thing, on purpose: *"if you get within the range of it, you cannot escape."*

**Both are right and the assertion must not be widened to cover this.** Keep `T22.11`'s ceiling
guard scoped to asteroid wells, and give the black hole its own inverse assertion: **from inside
the horizon, full thrust does not escape** — otherwise "you cannot escape" is a claim nothing
tests, and the first balance retune of the thruster quietly turns it into a hard-but-survivable
well. This is the third time in M22 that two true statements look like a contradiction; the
other two are `T22.05A`'s closure against `T22.10`'s breach, and they are all resolved the same
way — by scoping the claim, not by softening it.

## Two radii, and the inner one is a different kind of rule

- **The pull radius**, where it drags you: the same attractor `T22.11` sums and `T22.10`'s
  vortex reuses. **Three attractors now share one summation** — asteroid wells, the breach
  vortex, this. Do not write a third loop; the float-order fix, the prediction fix and the
  cutoff each have to be right once, not three times.
- **The event horizon**, where you die. Inside it the simulation stops asking what the player
  wants. That is not a strong force, it is a **state change** — modelling it as "a pull so
  strong you cannot win" leaves a player mashing thrust at a wall for several seconds, and
  makes the outcome depend on floating-point luck at the boundary.

## Read first
- `crates/game-core/src/effects/scheduler.rs` — `tick(now, round_ends_at)`, and the `ActiveEffect`
  lifecycle every current effect has
- `crates/game-core/src/map/carve.rs` — `Map::circle`, the one chokepoint (see `T22.10`)
- `crates/game-core/src/world/mod.rs` — `set_phase`, and T21.13's finding about `Ended`

## The scheduler does not do "permanent" or "at T-minus"

Every effect today is **interval-scheduled with a duration**: `roll_kind` picks, `next_at`
times it, and `active_duration(kind)` ends it. This one is neither — it is a **one-shot at a
point relative to round end**, and it **never ends**. `round_ends_at` is already a parameter of
`tick`, so the information is there; the lifecycle is not.

**Ruled: the round controller owns it** (`R21`) — and the reason is stronger than this file
states. The scheduler does not merely lack a permanent lifecycle, it **forbids one**: `tick`'s
start gate is `if now + EFFECT_TELEGRAPH + active_duration(kind) <= round_ends_at`, which a
permanent effect can never satisfy, and the comment beside it says why — *"an effect that
outlives the round would kill someone after the scoreboard is up."* Then
`self.active.retain(|e| e.phase != EffectPhase::Done)` requires an end.

The cost is an order of magnitude apart, counted: a fifth `EffectKind` touches 18 production
sites in `game-wasm/src/lib.rs`, 10 in `scheduler.rs`, 9 in `world/mod.rs`, 4 in
`game-server/src/config.rs`, plus `meteor.rs`, `lava.rs` and TypeScript string comparisons in
`render/weather-math.ts` and `ui/hud.ts`. The round controller is two `World` fields, one line
in `World::step`, one `state_hash` contribution, one line in the exhaustive destructure, one
event. **`World::round_ends_at()` is already computed and is the T-minus clock.**

**One trap on either route:** `World::step` calls `step_weather` only `if playing`, so nothing
ticks the hole in `Ended` — *"it stays drawn"* needs its own channel, a snapshot field or a
sticky client event.

## The carve is real, and mid-round

*"It will destroy one of the many random asteroids"* is a mask change during play. That path
exists — carves stream to clients by `seq` and `WorldMirror` buffers them — but:

- **The removed asteroid's gravity well goes with it** (`T22.11`). A well that outlives its rock
  pulls players toward nothing.
- **`T22.05B`'s destination picker must not offer the spot** afterwards, and neither must
  `T22.10`'s vortex. A player popped out inside a black hole is a death with no explanation.
- **Anyone standing on it** has to go somewhere. That is a decision, not a detail.
- **T21.13's lesson applies to the announcement**: `set_phase` early-returns when the phase
  already matches, and the `RoundState` push sat after that return — so round two announced
  nothing for the whole warmup and *nothing in the repo had ever asserted the message was
  sent*. Whatever announces the black hole needs a test that it was announced.

## What the owner still has to rule on

1. **Every round, or sometimes?** *"appears randomly at the last minute"* can mean the *time*
   within the last minute is random, or that it happens in some rounds and not others. Every
   round makes it a rule players can plan around; sometimes makes it a surprise. **Every round,
   random timing** is assumed below as the straight reading.
2. **Does it grow?** A fixed radius is a static hazard; a growing one is a closing circle and is
   what most of this genre does. The ask does not say.
3. **Can it eat a second asteroid?** It is permanent and it destroyed one on arrival — whether
   it keeps eating whatever drifts in follows naturally and is a much bigger feature.
4. **What happens at round end?** **Ruled in `R8.4`, and the analogy this file offers is
   wrong.** T21.30 froze *input* and deliberately kept **physics running** — its own comment:
   *"once the round is over, input does nothing — **but gravity does**."* `World::apply_inputs`
   in `Ended` substitutes a neutral `Input` and runs it through the same `apply_input`, so an
   attractor on that path **keeps pulling by default**. Gate it at the `Env` construction in
   `World::apply_inputs`, not at `set_phase` and not in the black hole's own code.

## Deliverable

- One black hole per round, arriving at a seeded random time inside the last minute, permanent
  for the rest of the round, in space only.
- It removes one asteroid on arrival, and everything that referenced that asteroid stops.
- A pull through the shared attractor; a horizon inside it that is a state change and kills.
- **A named death cause**, so the kill feed, the death overlay and the score all have something
  to say. "Died of nothing" is what a missing cause produces. **`R20` counts both ends, and
  the client end fails silently**: `GameScene`'s `death` handler narrows the wire string
  against an allowlist and anything unrecognised becomes `'player'`, so a new `"black_hole"`
  renders as *"? → ana (black_hole)"* — an unknown murderer, the exact bug
  `killfeed-state.test.ts` records having already been fixed once, for `void`. And note
  there is **no channel that carries a cause into `resolve_deaths`**: `Void` works by
  re-deriving from `is_in_the_void(p)`, deliberately — *"Derive, do not add a fourth flag."*
  Follow that shape or go through `DamageSource`.
- A shader worth looking at, with a Canvas fallback that is unmistakably the same event
  (T21.33, T21.36).

## Tests

- It arrives inside the last minute, over many seeds, and **never before** — with the control
  that it does arrive, or "never early" passes for a hazard that never spawns.
- Exactly one asteroid is gone afterwards, and its gravity well with it.
- **From inside the horizon, full thrust does not escape** — the inverse of `T22.11`'s guard,
  and the assertion that "you cannot escape" is a rule rather than a hope.
- A player outside the pull radius is unaffected — the control for the pull.
- Death is attributed: the cause reaches the feed and the overlay, asserted on **rendered
  pixels**, because a correct field that nothing draws is four shipped bugs' worth of precedent.
- `T22.11`'s asteroid-escape guarantee **still passes unchanged** — this task must not have
  widened it.
- Nothing happens in the other modes, with the presence control.

**Decisions (each reversible in one place, recorded at the code):**
- R8.4 says "zero the `accel`" at `Ended`; T22.11B's note there rules asteroid wells keep pulling, so only
  the **hole's** term is gated — `black_hole::pulling(hole, phase)`, called by `World::apply_inputs` and
  `GameCore::apply_input` alike. The horizon does not kill after the bell either.
- Arrival uniform over `[end − BLACK_HOLE_WINDOW, end − BLACK_HOLE_LATEST]` (60 s, 10 s): an arrival in the
  final instant is a hazard nobody meets (R8.1's own objection). Rolled on the first `Playing` tick on its own
  substream. The hole sits at the eaten rock's centre; the rock is carved (`CarveKind::Meteor`, radius `r + 2`).
- Sizes: horizon = `SPACE_ASTEROID_R_MAX`; capture (no direction escapes) = 2 × horizon; reach = 2 × capture;
  peak pull = 2 × (UP + SIDE thrust), so the pull equals the thrust bound exactly at the capture radius.
  **Thrust is anisotropic**, so sideways the pull already wins out to ≈213 px — the capture radius is where
  *no* direction escapes, not the only place you are lost (measured: 1.3 × capture sideways still died).
- Anyone standing on the eaten rock is inside the capture radius and goes in. Respawns and vortex trips are
  held outside `BLACK_HOLE_REACH` (the shared pickers take a filter; `choose_respawn`/`choose_respawn_pad`
  delegate with an accept-all one and draw exactly what they drew).
- **It never eats the last asteroid**: `Map::space_geometry()` is *derived from the asteroid list being
  non-empty*, so eating the only rock would switch off the rim, the void and every space rule mid-round — a
  field meaning two things, reported not changed. A one-rock map gets the hole at the arena centre.
