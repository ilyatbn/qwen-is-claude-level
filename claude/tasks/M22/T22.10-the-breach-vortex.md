# T22.10 — The breach vortex

Milestone: M22 · Depends on: **T22.05A** (there has to be a boundary to break), and shares
`T22.05B`'s destination picker · Size: ~250 lines · Origin: new, 2026-09-18

Asked for: *"lets add a fun secret. if you make a hole in the outer layer of the map (that
wraps the boundaries, you can still destroy it obviously), it creates a vortex that sucks you
in and pops you back out in a random location on the map."*

## What this actually is, stated once

**The boundary is the only thing keeping players in the arena. A hole in it is a way out.**
This feature is what makes that not true: breaching the rim does not let you leave, it recycles
you. So the vortex is **containment, dressed as a reward** — which is exactly why it works as a
secret rather than as an exploit, and it is the reason to build it at all rather than making the
rim indestructible.

Say that in the code. A reader who thinks this is decoration will make the vortex optional, and
the first thing that happens is a player floating off the map.

## Two findings that change how this is written

**1. It contradicts `T22.05A`'s closure test, and the contradiction is only apparent.**
`T22.05A` asserts the boundary is **closed** and says *"a boundary with a gap is a map players
leave"* — and that is still right, because it is a claim about **generation**. This task makes
a hole at **runtime**. The two are different claims and both must survive:

- generation-time: the rim is closed, walked not sampled (`T22.05A`);
- runtime: the rim may be breached, and a breach is a vortex, not an exit (here).

**Write both sentences into both files.** A later reader who finds a closure test and a feature
that makes holes will otherwise assume one of them is a bug.

**2. `WALL_W` and `BEDROCK_H` mean the rim cannot be built the obvious way.**
`Map::circle` — the one function every carve goes through — clamps to
`(WALL_W, w - WALL_W - 1)` horizontally and `h - BEDROCK_H` vertically, and `carve_circle`'s
own doc says *"Bedrock and the side walls are never touched."* So there is **already** an
indestructible border, and if `T22.05A` reaches for it to build the rim, the owner's *"you can
still destroy it obviously"* is false by construction and this whole task is dead.

**The rim must be ordinary destructible rock.** Note also that a circle inscribed in a
rectangular map leaves `WALL_W` strips at the far left and right *outside* the circle, which
are indestructible and are not the rim — `T22.05A` should say what is in that space.

## Detection belongs in `Map::circle`, and nowhere else

There are at least **five** production carve sites — `weapons/explode.rs` (two),
`weapons/bullet.rs`, `weapons/flame.rs`, `world/tombstones.rs` — and they all funnel through
`Map::circle`. A breach check written at the explosion path misses four callers, and the fifth
is a tombstone, which nobody would think to test. **One chokepoint, or this ships half-working
and the half that works is the half you tried by hand.**

`CarveResult` already carries `pixels_removed`, `dirty_chunks` and `revealed`, so the shape for
returning "and this opened the rim" already exists. Extend it there rather than scanning the rim
on a timer: a per-tick scan of an arc is work every tick for an event that happens rarely.

## Reuse the teleport path — all of it except the destination

`world/teleport.rs` already owns this exact event and its traps:

- **`teleport::arrive`** does cooldown *and* re-bases the arming rule, *"one function rather
  than three writes at the call site, because they are one event and the third is the one a
  caller forgets."*
- **`World::fire_pads` assigns a fresh `Body`, not a position.** Its comment: *"carrying the old
  velocity through means a player who teleports while running arrives running and slides off the
  destination pad, which looks like the teleport put them in the wrong place."* **In zero-g this
  is far worse** — nothing damps velocity (`T22.03`), so a player who arrives at the speed the
  vortex pulled them in at keeps it forever.
- **Fuel survives the trip** on a pad, deliberately, because a plain reset handed out a free
  full tank and *"turned every pad into a refuelling station"*. A vortex must make the same
  decision consciously, and in a mode where fuel is the whole economy (`T22.04`) it matters more.

**`teleport::destination` is the one piece that does not fit**: it picks uniformly among *other
pads*. This needs a random *map position*, which is `T22.05B`'s spawn picker. **Share that
function** — a second "somewhere valid on this map" is a second answer that can disagree, and
the failure mode is a player popped inside rock.

## The vortex is the second attractor in the mode — share one

`T22.11` sums a pull from every asteroid every tick, with a cutoff radius, in a stable order,
on both sides. **The vortex is the same shape of thing with different numbers**, and building a
second summation beside it is the "share the guard, or share the function" rule broken at the
level that matters: the two would each need the float-order fix, the prediction fix and the
cutoff, and the second one to be written would get one of them wrong.

## The suck is the risky half

*"Sucks you in"* is a force over a distance, not a trigger volume. That means:

- It runs inside the simulation every tick, so it is in **`apply_input`'s predicted path** — the
  recurring M22 hazard. A pull the client does not apply identically ships as rubber-banding,
  not as a weaker pull.
- In zero-g there is no damping, so a pull that is slightly too strong does not feel strong, it
  makes the arena unplayable. Give it a measured basis, not a chosen number.
- Anything stored for it is hashed and joins the exhaustive destructure in `world/mod.rs`.

## What the owner still has to rule on

1. **Does the vortex catch a player wearing wings?** Wings refuse teleport pads and gun
   platforms (2026-09-16). A vortex that politely declines is not a vortex, so the straight
   answer is *yes, it catches everyone* — but it is the opposite of the rule just written, so
   it needs saying rather than assuming.
2. **One hole, or many?** Two breaches means two vortices, and whether a bigger hole is a
   stronger one. The cheap version is one active vortex at a time.
3. **Does the hole heal?** `fill_circle` exists and is documented as *"not used in v1 gameplay;
   exists for tests and tools"*. If it never heals, late rounds are a rim full of vortices.
4. **Do items and projectiles get pulled in too?** Visually it is strange if they do not; a
   rocket that curves into a vortex is also a balance change nobody asked for.
5. **Is it on the minimap?** It is a *secret*, which argues no — but a player who cannot find
   it again cannot use it deliberately.

## Deliverable

- A breach detected at the one carve chokepoint, not at the call sites.
- A vortex at the hole: visible, a pull over a radius, and a capture.
- Arrival at a random valid position through `T22.05B`'s picker, on a seeded sub-stream, with a
  fresh body and a cooldown so a player who drifts back does not loop.
- The pull applied identically on both sides.

## Tests

- **A breach makes a vortex**: carve the rim, assert one exists. Control — carve an island in
  the middle and assert none does, or the test passes for a game that spawns vortices anywhere.
- **All five carve paths open one**, asserted per path. This is the test that catches the
  guard living at one call site.
- **A captured player comes out somewhere valid**: inside the boundary, not in rock, over many
  seeds — a population claim, not one draw.
- **You cannot leave through the hole.** The point of the feature. Drive a player out through a
  breach and assert they are inside the arena a moment later.
- **Prediction agrees** through a capture, and the state hash is identical across runs on a
  fixed seed.
- **Rendered pixels** for the vortex itself, both render paths (T21.33, T21.36).
- `T22.05A`'s generation-time closure test still passes, untouched.
