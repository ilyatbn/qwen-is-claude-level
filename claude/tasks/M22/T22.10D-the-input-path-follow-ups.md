## Do these

### F4 (MED-HIGH, player-visible) — one policy for a burst
One 15-tick frame (`MAX_FRAME_DT` 0.25 s) sends 15 inputs into one server tick. The room accepted 8
(`Seat::accepted_this_tick` capped at `MAX_INPUT_QUEUE`) and dropped the **newest** 7, never re-sent;
the world kept the other 7 as a standing queue for the rest of the match (~117 ms of input delay, and
`World::fire` aims from the last consumed input, so every shot came from ~117 ms ago); when the gap
was reached the ack jumped 7 and the client snapped 7 ticks. The room dropped newest while
`World::apply_inputs` trimmed oldest — two policies.

**Ruling (coordinator):** one policy. The room accepts at least `ceil(MAX_FRAME_DT / SIM_DT)` (= 15)
per tick, **derived from the constants, never a literal**; the world drains any backlog above a small
target (≈2) back down — drop the oldest or consume 2/tick; pick one and say why at the constant.
Test at room level: the reviewer's scenario — one 15-input burst, then 1/tick — ends with the queue
≤ target within a bounded number of ticks, no seq lost from the stream, no snap. Red before.

## As built (2026-09-24)
- **F4 — consume-twice against credit, not drop-oldest.** `constants.rs`: `MAX_FRAME_DT` 0.25
  (the client's `autoFire.ts` value, now pinned to it by `constants-parity.test.ts`),
  `MAX_FRAME_TICKS` = ceil(`MAX_FRAME_DT`·`SIM_HZ`) = 15, `MAX_INPUT_QUEUE` = `MAX_FRAME_TICKS`
  (was 8), `INPUT_BACKLOG_TARGET` 2. `World::apply_inputs` takes a second input in a tick while the
  player's backlog is above the target **and** it has credit — one per tick that passed with no
  input, capped at 15 (`World::input_credit`). Why not drop-oldest: a dropped input is travel the
  client predicted and the server never ran, i.e. a snap on every burst. Why the credit: without it
  consume-twice is §A30's exploit again (a client sending 2/tick moves 2×) — with it, inputs
  consumed never exceed ticks elapsed. Why 2, not 0: TCP delivers per-frame inputs in clumps, and
  two held back ride out a one-tick gap without a stop-and-double-step every remote would see.
  The room's per-tick cap is now only a flood guard; the backlog policy is the world's.
