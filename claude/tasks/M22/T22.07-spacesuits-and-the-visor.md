# T22.07 — Spacesuits, with a visor colour you choose

Milestone: M22 · Depends on: **T22.05B** (the mode that uses them), T20.04, T20.12 ·
Size: ~200 lines · Origin: the parked `T21.08-spacesuits.md`

Asked for: *"players are now in a spacesuit skin (make one with several colors and have a
different visor color selection)."*

## Two decisions this task makes rather than inherits

**1. Is a spacesuit a skin, or an override?** The brief says players *are* in a spacesuit in
this mode, which reads as a **mode override** — the game chooses it. But the visor colour is a
player choice. So it is both: a forced body with a chosen accent. Decide where each lives,
because a skin the player did not pick must not overwrite the skin they did. **Their chosen
skin must come back when the mode ends** — that is the regression this task is most likely to
ship, and nothing else will catch it.

**2. Where does the visor colour ride?** T20.12 ruled **new wire fields** over packing, on the
finding that `tombstone_skin_id` goes through join → `player_join`/`lobby_state` and **not**
into the replay. A visor colour is the same kind of thing: cosmetic, no simulation meaning.
Follow that ruling rather than re-deriving it.

## Notes

- **There is no spacesuit art and none is available.** The atlas is 5 characters × 10 poses,
  Kenney's pack has no accessories, and `../sprite_packs` is terrain and sky only.
  **Procedural, like the tombstones.**
- **Silhouette, not palette.** `tombstoneTextures.ts` states it: *"a skin picker whose options
  differ only by colour is a picker with one option."* This brief asks for colours, which is
  exactly the failure that rule names. **Give the suit a distinct silhouette**, and let colour
  distinguish players within it.
- **The skin count is six, not five** — true of the atlas (50 frames, five families), but
  `skins.json` declares six. Sizing anything off "5" is wrong by one.
- **`PlayerView` is rebuilt mid-round**, with `private readonly` appearance fields and no
  setter, so a suit applied once vanishes on the next rebuild. The solution is T20.12's
  `sameAppearance(...)` guard — **do not solve it a third time.** This task is that guard's
  first real test: `Appearance` grows from three fields to five, and its whole stated purpose is
  that *"the next accessory cannot be added to the map and the constructor and forgotten in the
  `if`."*
- **T21.37 is the Canvas half**: a tinted skin drew untinted on Canvas until a tint-multiplied
  atlas copy was baked per tint. A coloured visor on Canvas meets the same problem.

## The shipped violation that is a trap for your test

`assets/skins.json` declares **six skins over five distinct atlas prefixes** — skin 5
*"Recruit (red)"* is skin 0 tinted. **RULING: leave it, on scope.** But it is a live trap: a
test that happened to compare **0 against 5** would be measuring a *tint* while claiming to
measure a *sprite*, and it would pass weakly, forever.

**So: any skin-comparison assertion here must pick a pair with different atlas prefixes, and say
in a comment why that pair was chosen.**

## Tests

- **Rendered pixels, with a control**: two players with different visor colours differ in the
  same frame, and a control region neither occupies is unchanged.
- The suit is a distinct silhouette from a normal character — on pixels, not on an id.
- **A player's chosen skin returns when the mode ends.**
- Visor persistence round-trips through `readId`'s `String(n) === raw` check; junk refused.
- Canvas and WebGL both (T21.37).
