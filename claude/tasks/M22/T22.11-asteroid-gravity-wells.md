# T22.11 — Asteroid gravity wells

Milestone: M22 · Depends on: **T22.05A** (asteroids, and their levels) · Interacts hard with
**T22.03** and **T22.04** · Size: **large** · Origin: new, 2026-09-18

Asked for: *"each 'island' (lets make it an asteroid since we're in space) has its own internal
gravity. it pulls players towards it. they have several levels of gravity, lets say 1-5. its
harder to escape from them."*

## This retires "zero gravity" as a description of the mode

There is no *global* gravity — that part of `T22.03` stands. But the mode is not weightless: it
is **many local wells**, and a player is always being pulled by something. That is a different
feel and a different physics problem, and the milestone's language has been updated to say so.
`T22.03` still owns momentum, collisions and fuel; **this task owns the field**.

## The seam does not exist, and that is the whole difficulty

Measured 2026-09-18, not remembered:

```rust
pub fn apply_gravity(body: &mut Body, gravity_scale: f32, dt: f32) {
    if gravity_scale == 0.0 { return; }
    body.vel.y += GRAVITY * gravity_scale * dt;
    if body.vel.y > MAX_FALL_SPEED { body.vel.y = MAX_FALL_SPEED; }
}
```

Three things follow, and each is a decision this task has to make:

1. **Gravity is a scalar applied to `vel.y`.** `gravity_scale` cannot express *"towards that
   point"*. Every existing caller — `integrate(map, body, gravity_scale, dt)`,
   `jetpack::gravity_scale`, `T22.02`'s low-gravity multiplier — is on the scalar path. **A
   vector field is a new argument or a new function, and the choice between them is the
   single largest design call in M22.** Adding a `Vec2` beside the scalar means two things
   answer "what accelerates this body"; replacing the scalar with a vector touches every
   caller in the tree, including standard gravity.
2. **`MAX_FALL_SPEED` clamps `vel.y` only.** A sideways pull is unclamped today, and in a mode
   with no damping (`T22.03`) an unclamped acceleration is unbounded speed. **There is no
   terminal velocity in this mode until this task writes one**, and it cannot be the existing
   constant because that one is vertical.
3. **`apply_gravity` early-returns at scale 0.** That is the path wings take and the path
   `T22.03` builds on, so today the flying regime skips gravity entirely. The wells have to be
   applied somewhere that is *not* skipped by that return.

## "Levels 1–5" wants a stated basis, not five numbers

A level is a multiplier on a pull, and the thing that makes levels meaningful is **escape
velocity**: `T22.04`'s thruster has a finite delta-v per unit of fuel, and level 5 is
*"harder to escape"* exactly insofar as it costs more of it.

**So the five levels are derived from the thruster, not chosen independently.** State the
relation — level *n* costs roughly *k(n)* fuel to leave from the surface — and put the basis in
the constant's doc comment the way `capacity.rs::max_rooms_carries_its_basis` does. Five
literals with no relation to the fuel tank is a table nobody can retune, and this project has
measured what that costs: a suite pinned to a constant cannot detect the constant moving.

**Scope the guard to asteroids.** `T22.12`'s black hole is deliberately inescapable, and the
owner asked for it in those words. Widening this assertion to cover every attractor in the mode
would make the two tasks contradict each other; `T22.12` carries the inverse assertion instead.

**The hazard at the top of the range: a well nobody can escape.** If level 5's pull exceeds
what a full tank can overcome, a player who touches it is dead with no cause on screen and no
message. **Assert the ceiling** — maximum well against maximum thrust — as a compile-time or
test-time guard, the way `boots_fall_safe_speed`'s relation to `MAX_FALL_SPEED` is asserted.

## The field is summed every tick, on both sides, in the same order

*"Tons of tiny islands"* (`T22.05A`) means N is large. Consequences, all of them load-bearing:

- **Float summation is not associative.** Client and server must add the same contributions in
  the same order or the two drift apart — silently, and worse in a mode where nothing damps the
  error. Iterate a sorted, stable list; never a `HashMap`, for the reason `World::players`
  already gives.
- **It is in `apply_input`'s predicted path**, which is the recurring M22 hazard: a field the
  client computes differently ships as rubber-banding.
- **Cost.** N wells × M players × 60 Hz. A cutoff radius per asteroid is the obvious answer and
  it is also a *gameplay* decision — a well with a hard edge is a wall you fall off.

## The question that changes the art as well as the physics

**Does a player pulled toward an asteroid's centre *orient* to its surface?**

- If **yes**, "down" is local and everything drawn upright is wrong: the walk cycle, the weapon
  flip (`facingLeft(aim)` and the vertical mirror `playerView.ts` documents), the hat and
  glasses, the name label, the health bars. That is a rendering project of its own.
- If **no**, players stay upright and are simply pulled — which the owner's reference image
  supports, since every astronaut in it is upright on a rock whatever side they are on.

**No is assumed below** because it is what the picture shows and it is a tenth of the work, but
this is the owner's call and it is the expensive one to get wrong.

## Does the field touch anything but players?

Projectiles curving around asteroids would look extraordinary and is a real balance change —
`weapons/projectile.rs` carries a per-projectile `gravity_scale`, and bullets are `0.0` so they
would be unaffected while grenades curve. Items, crates and tombstones all fall today. **Rule
on each; do not discover them.**

## Deliverable

- A gravity level, 1–5, per asteroid — generated in `T22.05A`, hashed, and on the wire, since
  the client predicts against it.
- A summed field applied to players, deterministic and order-stable.
- A terminal speed for this mode that is not `MAX_FALL_SPEED`.
- The level→pull relation derived from the thruster's delta-v, with the basis written down.

## Tests

- A player near a level-*n* asteroid accelerates toward it, and one far away does not — the
  control, or the test passes for a field that pulls everything everywhere.
- **Escape is possible from every level on a full tank**, asserted at the ceiling.
- Level 5 is measurably harder to escape than level 1 — a ratio, against the stated basis.
- Two asteroids pull a player between them and the result is the sum, in a fixed order.
- Prediction agrees with the server after drifting through a field, and the state hash is
  identical across runs on a fixed seed.
- **Standard-gravity modes are untouched** — the control that this did not change the game
  everyone else is playing.
