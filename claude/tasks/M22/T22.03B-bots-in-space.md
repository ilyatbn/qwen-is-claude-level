## What T22.03 already did to this file, and it is not the locomotion

**One edit, forced by a compile-time trap, and it is done:** `bots::zone_reach` **divides** by
`GravityMode::scale()`, and the `const _: () = assert!(… Space.scale() > 0.0 …)` at the top of
`bots/mod.rs` existed to fail the build the day that became `0.0`. **It fired.** The flame arm
now takes the lesser of the ballistic range and the flame's own lifetime range:

```rust
let ballistic = speed * speed / (GRAVITY * FLAME_GRAVITY_SCALE * gravity.scale());
Some(ballistic.min(speed * FLAME_LIFE) + FLAME_RADIUS)
```

The `const _` is kept, pointed at what still has to hold — `GRAVITY > 0.0 &&
FLAME_GRAVITY_SCALE > 0.0`, the half of the division no mode can change.

**And that produced a number this task owns.** At `MOLOTOV_FLAME_SPEED` 220 and `FLAME_LIFE`
5 s the zero-g reach is **≈1100 px**, against ≈99 px under standard gravity. A Small map is
2048 px wide. `bots::stand_off` feeds that into the approach guard, so **a bot carrying a
molotov in space keeps a stand-off it can essentially never satisfy and never throws it.**
The number is physically right — a flame that never falls really does travel that far — so the
fix is a *behavioural* decision, not a formula correction. Decide it here.

## The locomotion, which is the task

`M22-RULINGS` R5, in full: bots get a space locomotion layer. **Target selection, aiming and
firing are untouched** — only the part that turns *"I want to be over there"* into inputs
changes:

- thrust toward the desired direction;
- burn fuel like a player;
- **stop thrusting when the velocity already points where they want to go** — nothing damps,
  so a bot that thrusts whenever it is not at its destination overshoots every time and
  oscillates;
- **thrust against their own velocity when it does not** — braking is a thrust here, and it is
  the manoeuvre a walking model has no concept of.

**Not optional.** `balance.rs` measures a mode where bots are inert as a game with no fights,
and a mode nobody can playtest alone is a mode nobody playtests.

## What the space regime actually gives a bot, so this is not rediscovered

Read `player/space.rs`'s module doc first; it is the contract. The parts that decide this
task:

- **A held direction is the throttle.** In space `apply_input` engages the jetpack on
  `UP`/`DOWN`/`LEFT`/`RIGHT` while `space::floating`, not on `JUMP`. A bot that presses JUMP
  in mid-air in space burns nothing and goes nowhere. **This is the single thing a walking
  model gets wrong** — `bots/mod.rs`'s jetpack climb gate emits `JUMP | UP`.
- **`JUMP` is the push off a rock, and it costs `SPACE_JUMP_FUEL`.** It only launches while
  grounded, and it is *refused* when the tank cannot pay.
- **Walking on a rock is free and ordinary.** A grounded bot in space walks at `WALK_SPEED`
  with friction, coyote time and the jump buffer, exactly as it does anywhere else (R4).
- **`grounded` means what it always meant, and it is now reachable again.** R4's probe grounds
  a body that drifts sideways onto the top of an asteroid, so a bot that lands can walk.

## The three `me.body.grounded` reads, cited by symbol

All three are inside the movement step of `bots/mod.rs`; find them with
`grep -n "me.body.grounded" crates/game-core/src/bots/mod.rs` rather than by line, per
`CLAUDE.md`:

1. the stuck-jump `wants_jump` — `self.still_for > STUCK_WINDOW || (rise > STEP_UP as f32 &&
   me.body.grounded)`. In space "stuck" against a rock is resolved by thrusting *away*, not by
   jumping;
2. the jetpack climb gate — `rise > JETPACK_RISE && me.jetpack.fuel > JETPACK_MAX_FUEL * 0.5`,
   which emits `JUMP | UP`. In space the `JUMP` bit is dead weight and `UP` is the whole
   instruction;
3. the descend gate — `rise < -JETPACK_RISE * 2.0 && !me.body.grounded`. In space there is no
   falling, so descending is `DOWN` held and paid for.

## Hazards

- **Do not fork the movement step.** One path, branching on the mode, the same rule T22.03
  followed for `apply_input`.
- **A bot that never stops thrusting is worse than a bot that never moves.** The overshoot
  oscillation is the characteristic failure of this model and it will look like "the bots are
  jittering", not like "the bots cannot navigate". Assert on **arrival** — distance to the
  destination after N ticks — and not on "a thrust was emitted", which is the
  intention-not-effect shape.
- **`bots::zone_reach`'s 1100 px, above.** Decide and record.
- **Fuel is the whole economy** and bots share the player's tank rules. A bot that thrusts
  flat out arrives dry and cannot fight. `JETPACK_CLIMB_BUDGET` already exists as the
  precedent for a bot reasoning about its tank.

## Tests

- A bot in space **reaches a destination** it is given, across seeds, and a control under
  standard gravity reaches the same one — so the assertion measures the space layer and not
  the pathing.
- A bot **arrives and stops**: the velocity at arrival is small, or the overshoot oscillation
  ships.
- A bot **thrusts against its own velocity** when the destination is behind it. Falsify by
  deleting the brake.
- `balance.rs` reports a space round with fights in it, aggregated across seeds — the
  measurement R5 names, with a standard-gravity control on the same instrument.

  √(2·BOT_SPACE_BRAKE·d))`, or out of a hazard at cruise) and the per-axis error against the body's velocity, thrust
  past `BOT_SPACE_DEADBAND`; the brake is the error's sign flipping. Grounded: walks (free) and lifts off with `UP`,
