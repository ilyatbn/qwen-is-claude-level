## As built
- **`SPACE_IRON_MIN_OVER_ORDINARY` = 1.125** (9/8, so `ceil` is exact in f32). One spelling of the floor,
  `space::iron_floor(largest_ordinary)` = `ceil(1.125 × r)`, used by `cap_iron_share` (its `r_min`, was T22.22B's
  largest + 1) and by both tests.
- **Small's iron band 90..100 → 99..100** (`SPACE_IRON_R_MIN_FRAC_SMALL` 1.125 → **1.2375** = 1.125 × 88 / 80): the band
  now starts at the floor for the largest ordinary rock *any* map can hold (`grown_radius(80, 0.2)` = 88), so no iron
  is ever drawn under R113b's ratio; `every_space_map_seats_its_iron…` asserts every scale's band floor ≥
  `iron_floor(88)` = 99 and every iron ≥ `iron_floor(its map's largest)`. Medium/Large (120..160) untouched.
- **Share cap per scale: `ScaleParams::iron_share_max`** — `SPACE_IRON_SHARE_MAX` 0.35 on Medium/Large,
  **`SPACE_IRON_SHARE_MAX_SMALL` 0.44** on Small (basis table in its doc: 999-seed max 42.9 % + 1.1 points margin).
- **Decision for the coordinator — the generator still aims every map at 0.35; 0.44 is the bound, not the target.**
  `cap_iron_share` shrinks iron while the map is over 0.35, down to R113b's floor; a map whose iron hits the floor
  ships over 0.35 (the owner's "larger" wins), and only such maps exceed it — 101 of 999 Small maps, none on Medium or
  Large. Measured the literal alternative (shrink only to the loosened 0.44): **676 of 999 Small maps over 0.35**, per-map
  p50 34.8 → 36.6 %, p95 36.3 → 43.2 % — it loosens every map, not "only as far as needed". Reversible: pass the scale's
  `iron_share_max` into `cap_iron_share`.
- **The fallback (shrink the ordinary rocks) was not needed**: with the bound measured at the floor, both constraints
  hold on all 2997 sweep maps. Ordinary rocks are untouched.
- Still **2** iron per map (asserted, 40 seeds × 3 scales). `REPLAY_VERSION` 36 → **37**.
- **Fixture moved by the map:** `black_hole::tests::at_ended_it_freezes_and_stays` put its body 0.6 × reach sideways of
  seed 9 Small's hole — now 48 px inside an iron rock (r 80), held still, so its control read *"the hole did not pull
  (0, 0)"*. Its premise was only "inside the arena"; it now takes the first point on the same ring whose body box
  (± `PLAYER_W`/`PLAYER_H`) is air inside the arena, and its control checks the pull *toward the hole* (it was `x < -1`,
  which only holds for the sideways point). Outside Touch only — the one test in the lib run the new arena broke.
