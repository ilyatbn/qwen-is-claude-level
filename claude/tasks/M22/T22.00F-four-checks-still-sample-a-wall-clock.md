**exactly as T22.00B's own report predicted**

`T22.00B` fixed `smoke-shader`, which decided whether a shader animates by sampling **two frames
across a wall clock**. Its report named the four checks still doing it and they were left alone:
**`fire-shader`, `explosion-shader`, `beams-shader`, `fog-shader`.** `tasks/flaky-test.md` records
that sentence.

On 2026-09-22 `beams-shader` went red on an **idle** box:

```
held beam, 200 ms apart: stroked 0.0% of pixels changed, painted 0.9%
FAIL: the painted beam changed 0.9% of its pixels in 200 ms against 0.0% for the still
      strokes — it does not animate
```

The assertion is `living > Math.max(0.01, still * 3)`. It needed **1.0%** and got **0.9%** — a
10% margin on a hardcoded threshold over a fixed 200 ms window. Five of the check's other
assertions passed in the same run, including the two that prove the painted beam is on screen and
differs from the stroked one. **The shader is fine; the instrument is not.**

This is not a new diagnosis. It is `T22.00B`'s, written down before the failure happened, and the
failure arrived where it said it would.

## The fix, and why it is not "raise the threshold"

`CLAUDE.md`: **a gate that fails on a coin flip gates nothing — remove such an assertion or fix its
cause; do not weaken it.** Raising the threshold or lengthening the window buys a bigger margin on
the same broken instrument, and `T22.00B` measured that a 300 ms window is marginal *regardless of
load* (39 idle samples ranged 0.0–11.2%). **Do not tune the number.**

Count **drawn frames**, not milliseconds — that is what `smoke-shader` does now. Sample across a
known number of rendered frames and take the **maximum** change observed, so a sample that lands on
two frames of the same animation phase does not decide the run.

**And carry over `T22.00B`'s second assertion, which is the half that makes the first one mean
something.** Its two falsifications, both at the live binding site, are the model:
- pinning `time` to 0 in the shader gives *"changed 0.0 % of its pixels at most over 90 drawn
  frames (1.6 s) … it does not animate"* — the real bug;
- killing `requestAnimationFrame` gives *"the page drew 0 of 90 frames in 40.0 s … the box stopped
  rendering, so this says nothing about whether the shader animates"* — **the distinction the
  broken check could not make, and the reason it produced two false sightings.**

A check that cannot tell *"the shader is frozen"* from *"the page stopped drawing"* reports the
second as the first. Each of your four must make that distinction and say so in its own message.

## Outcome (builder, 2026-09-22)

Done. All four step **5 x 18 drawn frames** and take the largest change; **no threshold moved**.
Idle, on the tree committed: beams **19.3 %** (the assertion that read 0.9 % in the gate),
explosion **57.8 %**, fire **13.8 %**, fog drift **98.70** — each against an untouched floor,
each with 90/90 frames drawn and a 0.0 % still control.

**Two falsifications each, at the live binding site, with different messages.** Pinning `time`
in the fragment shader (`shaders.ts`) reds the animation claim naming that shader; replacing the
page's `requestAnimationFrame` reds the renderer claim instead — *"the page drew 0 of 90 frames
in 40.0 s … the box stopped rendering, so this says nothing about whether the &lt;X&gt; shader
animates"*. See the journal entry for the pastes.

**`fog-shader` needed a different frozen-clock plant, and the first one is worth keeping.**
`float time = 0.0` in `FOG_FRAGMENT` also collapses the fog's *spatial* structure — 47.8 → 14.7
against a 4x-of-4.4 bar — so the check reds one assertion earlier, on `VARIETY_RATIO`, and never
reaches the drift claim. Pinning the derived phase instead (`float t = 3.7`) keeps the structure
and kills only the motion, and then the drift assertion fires: *"the shader fog moved only 0.00
at most over 90 drawn frames (3.0 s)"*. **A frozen clock and a still picture are not the same
plant here**, which is the sort of thing only running it tells you.

### The 0.783-vs-0.794 red is **`fog-visible`, not `fog-shader`** — a different defect

Measured, not guessed. `fog-shader` has no assertion of that shape: its veil self-validation
logs `flat veil: alpha 0.800, ground contrast surviving 0.208 (expected 0.200)` in all ten gate
logs in the tree, and its tolerance is 0.06. The pair 0.783/0.794 belongs to
`fog-visible.mjs`'s ramp assertion — `Math.abs(s.a - k.FOG_SCREEN_ALPHA * s.s) > 0.01`, where
`0.8 x 0.993 = 0.794` and the tree's own logs read `veil alpha 0.785` and `0.798`.

**And it is not a two-frame wall-clock sample at all.** It is one snapshot compared against
itself across two different clocks: `fogStrength` is computed live at the moment `debug()` is
called (`GameScene.ts`, `fogStrength: self.fog.strength(self.roundTime)`) while `fogAlpha`
returns `weather.ts::lastFogAlpha`, written at the **last drawn frame**. The poll breaks at
`s.s >= 0.99`, i.e. deliberately **while the ramp is still climbing**, so the two are a frame
apart on a value that is moving — at the 13–20 fps these logs show, one frame of `FOG_RAMP` is
worth several times the 0.01 tolerance. Frames-plus-steps does not fix that; sampling both ends
from one drawn frame does. **Not fixed here** — outside Touch only, and it is `fog-visible`'s.
