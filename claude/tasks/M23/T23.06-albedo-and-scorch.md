# T23.06 — Painting the rock: albedo and relief on the GPU, scorch from explosions

Milestone: M23 · Depends on: T23.03, T23.05 · Size: ~250 lines · Rulings: R4, R5

## Do
1. Upload T23.05's fields into **one world-sized RGBA8 texture** (`texSubImage2D` on the dirty rect only). One
   texture, not per-chunk: the shader reads ~50 px away and chunk seams would show.
2. Port `world.js`'s albedo painting (strata, grain, Worley boulders, pebbles, cracks, soil and grass on upward-facing
   edges, grass fringe into the air) to a GPU pass that writes a world-sized albedo texture per dirty rect. The hash
   is integer (`Math.imul`) and **ports bit-exact to WebGL2 `uint`** — do that, so the look-lab reproduces F1's rock
   texel for texel.
3. **One palette, no themes (R5):** the albedo takes its colours from `P.terrain` only. Stop reading `MapMeta.theme`
   on the client for terrain.
4. **Scorch**: the mockup's static `spec.scorch` list becomes an accumulated scorch-mask texture written by the
   client's existing explosion events.
5. Hook carve events to dirty-rect updates within today's `CHUNK_REBAKE_MS` budget; measure and paste.

## Tests
- vitest: the ported hash equals the mockup's JS hash over 10k inputs.
- Browser check `look-albedo`: look-lab F1 with lighting forced flat (albedo only) vs the same render from the mockup
  harness with lighting flat — Level A within the floor (it should be near-identical; say the number).
- Carving in the sandbox updates only the dirty rect (count texels changed outside it: 0), and the crater shows
  scorch where the explosion was (presence) and nowhere else (absence, with the pre-carve frame as control).
