# T20.20 — A "flake" that is a fixture defect (v8)

Milestone: M20 · Depends on: nothing · Size: small — **but the finding is the deliverable**

**Found by T20.11's coder while diagnosing a red gate.** It is listed as a D-58 flake. **It is
not a flake.** It has a mechanism, and the mechanism is in the fixture.

## What was found

`crates/game-server/tests/room.rs::commands_sent_between_ticks_are_all_applied` failed with
*"moved the player from 2032 to 2032"* — and passes **20/20 alone**, which is what put it on
the flaky list.

But it is not load-sensitive:

- `test_config()` has **`fixed_seed: None`**, so the spawn point varies run to run.
- **2032.0 is exactly `MAP_SMALL_W − WALL_W − PLAYER_W/2`** — the world's right wall.
- At that x, `clamp_to_world` also clamps `vel.x ≤ 0`. **So holding RIGHT is a no-op by
  construction**, and the test fails whenever the seed happens to spawn the player there.

A test that fails on a spawn position is not flaky; it is **under-specified**, and it has been
absorbing a real failure into a flaky-list entry that told everyone to re-run it.

## The answer already exists, one language over

`scripts/checks/audio.mjs` hit this exact problem and solved it with **`roomFor(dir)`** —
check there is room to move in the direction you are about to push, and choose the direction
that has it. **The Rust fixture has not learned it.** Port the idea; do not invent a second
one.

## Deliverable

- The fixture chooses a direction with room, or pins a seed that guarantees it — **and says
  which and why.** A pinned seed is the weaker answer: it makes the test pass without making
  it correct, and the next map change re-rolls the problem.
- **Remove it from D-58's table**, with a line saying what it actually was. An entry that says
  "flaky, re-run it" on a test with a deterministic cause trains everyone to ignore a real red.

## Notes

- **Check the rest of D-58's table the same way.** This one was found because somebody asked
  *why* rather than re-running. `sigterm_leaves_a_verifiable_file…` is a genuine member (15/15
  alone). The others have not been examined for a mechanism — and this task's whole point is
  that "passes alone" is a symptom, not a diagnosis.
- The `2032.0` coincidence is what cracked it: a number that looks like a position turning out
  to be a boundary derived from three constants. **Compute suspicious literals against the
  constants before assuming they are data.**

## THE SAME FINDING AT FILE SCOPE — three timeout budgets are the constants they wait on

Found by the D-58 sweep this task prompted. **The tell is identical: a literal that looks like
a timeout and is actually a shipped constant.**

    rooms.rs::BUDGET_MS       = 10_000     LOBBY_BOT_TIMEOUT  = 10.0    WARMUP_SECONDS = 10.0
    in_progress.rs::BUDGET_MS = 30_000     READY_TIMEOUT_SECS = 30.0    ROOM_EMPTY_TTL = 30.0
    lobby.rs::BUDGET_MS       = 30_000

**So every `wait_for` in those files gives up at precisely the tick on which the room it is
watching changes state under it** — seats bots, ends warmup, sweeps the unready, or becomes
reapable. Under load the wait approaches its budget, and *at* the budget it is racing a state
transition rather than merely being slow.transition rather than merely being slow.

> because most of today's re-validations found errors and this one did not:
>
> | claim | measured |
> |---|---|
> | `rooms.rs::BUDGET_MS` = `LOBBY_BOT_TIMEOUT` = `WARMUP_SECONDS` | `10_000` ms = `10.0` = `10.0` ✓ |
> | `lobby.rs`/`in_progress.rs::BUDGET_MS` = `READY_TIMEOUT_SECS` = `ROOM_EMPTY_TTL` | `30_000` ms = `30.0` = `30.0` ✓ |
> | 2032.0 is the right wall | `MAP_SMALL_W 2048 − WALL_W 8 − PLAYER_W/2 8` = **2032.0** ✓ |
>
> `MAP_SMALL_W` is the right width to use because `test_config` sets
> `map_scale: MapScale::Small`. And 2032 appears **nowhere** as a literal in `crates/` — it is
> an observed runtime position, which is what the diagnosis requires it to be.
>
> **Two citation defects fixed, both of the kind that make a builder think the finding is
> stale.** The test was cited as `src/room.rs`; it lives in **`tests/room.rs`**. `src/room.rs`
> is the 2800-line room implementation, so grepping it for the test name returns nothing and
> looks like the test was deleted.
>
> **And `fixed_seed: None` is inherited, not written.** It arrives through
> `..Config::default()`, with the `None` in `config.rs`'s `Default` impl. Check it with
> `awk '/fn test_config/,/^}/' <file> | grep -c fixed_seed`, which returns **0** for all eight
> copies. Do **not** use `grep -rn fixed_seed crates/game-server/tests/` for this — an earlier
> draft of this box claimed that returns 0 and it returns **6**.
>
> **Those six are the most useful thing in this section: the fix already exists in-tree, six
> times over.** `replay.rs:65,317,395,552` set `fixed_seed: Some(4242)` and
> `replay_run.rs:52,661` set `Some(90210)` and `Some(31337)` — and **neither file defines a
> `test_config` at all**. So the seed discipline is established practice here; it simply stops
> at the `test_config` boundary, and the eight fixtures that most need it are the eight that
> inherit `None`. Copy the shape from `replay.rs` rather than inventing one, and note that
> those files pin the seed *inline at the call site*, which is why they never acquired the
> shared fixture this task is about. **Two of D-58's four entries live in `rooms.rs` and
one in `in_progress.rs`.**

That the numbers coincide is **measured and exact**. That the coincidence **causes** the
failures is **SUSPECTED** — and the experiment is unusually clean:

**Set `rooms.rs::BUDGET_MS` to `25_000` — a value that is not any constant — and re-run
`cargo test -p game-server`.** If the failures stop moving, the budget was the mechanism and
**three of D-58's four entries need no change at all.** If they continue, record it as
examined-and-exonerated. **Run this before touching any of those four tests.**

Either way the budgets must stop being bare literals. `scripts/lib/rust-constants.mjs` exists
so a *browser* fixture can pin to a constant; **the Rust side has no equivalent, and these
three files are why it needs one.**

## Two more entry-specific defects, neither load

- **`rooms.rs::two_clients_in_one_room_do_hear_each_other`** — `wait_for(…, 1, …)` guarantees
  **`>= 1`**, then a later `count()` is asserted **`== 1`**. Different claims, with an
  unbounded gap between the lines. Not reachable today at `bot_count: 0`, so latent — but it
  is a weaker-guarantee-than-assertion shape that breaks the day anything else joins, **and it
  will present as a flake.**
- **`in_progress.rs::quick_match_makes_a_new_lobby_rather_than_being_refused`** — asserts
  `count(join_error) == 0` with **no presence control**. Checked and it is *not* vacuous —
  `connect()` does subscribe to `join_error` — but it still cannot distinguish "none was sent"
  from "one was sent after we sampled", because the sample is taken the instant a *different*
  event lands. Provoke a real `join_error` and assert the counter sees it.
- **`replay_run.rs::sigterm_leaves_a_verifiable_file_and_sigkill_does_not`** — **examined, no
  deterministic cause found.** Its `recv_timeout(10s)` is the socket.io `open` handshake, not
  a game boundary, and no literal in it resolves to a constant. Four real-process waits in
  series that compound under a full-workspace run. **Recorded so nobody re-derives it.**

## Tests

- The fixture must fail if the movement it asserts genuinely stops working — falsify by
  breaking the command path, not by changing the spawn.
- A control that the chosen direction really does have room, so the fix cannot silently become
  "push the way that happens to work".

## Independent corroboration, from a task that was not looking for it

**Reported by the T20.19 coder 2026-09-06, not yet verified by me** — the tree was dirty and
the gate running, so this is its account and is marked as such until the commit lands. It is
recorded because it arrived from a direction that could not have been fitted to this
diagnosis: T20.19 is a client-prediction fix and its author was building a fixture, not
investigating a flake.

Building a fixture that walks a player, it hit **the same spawn-against-a-wall failure twice,
on two different maps and in two languages**:

- **Rust, seed 4242:** player 1 seats at **x = 16**, against the *left* wall, on a slope. At
  reduced health, `WALK_SPEED × HEALTH_SPEED_MIN` does not move the body at all. A mask search
  reportedly found **no level clear stretch anywhere on that map**, so the fixture had to
  *build* its shelf rather than find one.
- **TypeScript:** `spawn_points[0]` travels 41 px right and stops dead at `vx === 0`, so that
  fixture needed a `roomFor(dir)` probe; LEFT was clear.

**Why this matters here.** This task's diagnosis is that `commands_sent_between_ticks_are_all_applied`
fails when the seed spawns a player at **x = 2032**, the right wall, where `clamp_to_world`
holds `vel.x ≤ 0`. The corroboration is the *same mechanism at the opposite wall, on a
different seed, reached by someone not looking for it* — which is much better evidence than a
second failure of the same test would have been.

**It also widens the scope.** The flake is not one unlucky test. **Any fixture that walks a
player on an unseeded map is exposed**, and the T20.19 author had to solve it twice before
getting to the thing they were actually testing. That is the cost of the eight `test_config`
copies inheriting `fixed_seed: None` (T20.18), paid by a task with no connection to either.

**Verify before relying on this**, per the box above: re-read the T20.19 fixtures once
committed and confirm the two accounts at the code.
