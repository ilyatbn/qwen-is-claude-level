# T20.24 — The server runs out of rooms at 48 players (v8)

Milestone: M20 · Depends on: T20.22, T20.23 · Size: **measure first, then a design decision**

**Found by T20.14's load driver.** This is a bottleneck, not a crash, and the first job is to
decide how much of it is the two leaks above and how much is the design.

## The measurement

`cargo run -p game-server --example loadgen -- --clients 48 --seconds 60`, box load average
1.24 at the start:

```
10634 join verbs, 172 welcome, 10460 join_error
  {"full": 22, "in_progress": 2069, "server_full": 8367, "unknown_code": 2}
rooms: peak 32/32 over 239 samples; 32 mid-run, 32 at the end
worst room tick 8.56 ms; 1 room(s) over half the 16.67 ms budget
```

**`MAX_ROOMS` is the binding resource, and it is not CPU.** 32 rooms × `MAX_PLAYERS` 6 is 192
seats; 48 clients exhausted the room table, and 79 % of join attempts were refused
`server_full` while no room's worst tick ever reached 9 ms of the 16.67 ms budget.

**Read that last number narrowly.** `room_health` is a lifetime running maximum, not a rate
(T20.25), so it answers *"did any room ever come near the budget"* — which is the claim made
here — and it cannot answer *"does per-room cost grow with the room count"*. That second
question belongs to `capacity.rs::rooms_do_not_get_more_expensive_as_more_are_added`, which
is `#[ignore]`d and runs through `scripts/ignored.sh`.

The verb *rate* is the driver's own behaviour — a client refused `server_full` retries at
once — so read the ratio and the room count, not the 170 verbs/s.

## Three candidate causes, in the order they should be measured

1. **Public lobbies auto-start and then become un-joinable.** `room.rs` sets
   `starts_in = config.lobby_bot_timeout` (**10 s**) on the first seating of a non-private
   room, and `registry.rs::quick_match` skips any room where `handle.has_started()`. So ten
   seconds after the first arrival a lobby stops accepting anyone and the next quick match
   mints another room. A steady arrival stream therefore creates rooms at roughly the arrival
   rate and frees them only `ROOM_EMPTY_TTL` (**30 s**) after they empty.
2. **T20.23's unreapable rooms**, which never free at all.
3. **T20.22's orphaned seats**, which make rooms look fuller than they are.

None of the three is CPU, which is why raising the tick budget or the worker count is not on
this list.

Fix 2 and 3 first and re-measure before touching 1 — the loadgen soak is the instrument, and
its room-count line is the number to compare.

## What a decision here has to answer

- Is `MAX_ROOMS = 32` the right number, and what is it protecting? `capacity.rs`'s
  `max_rooms_carries_its_basis` pins a claim about it; read that before changing it.
- Should `quick_match` be allowed to seat into a *warmup* match, or should a lobby stop
  auto-starting while a join is in flight? Note `docs/74` §E4 forbids the first.
- Is refusing `server_full` the right client experience, or should there be a queue?

**Do not raise `MAX_ROOMS` as the fix.** It moves the wall without measuring what walked into
it, and the two leaks above would refill any budget given.

## Notes

- The load driver's own limits are stated in its module doc: no browser, no packet loss, one
  process, no bots. In particular it cannot say anything about memory or file descriptors,
  which `tasks/HANDOFF-M19.md` lists among the unexcluded variables.
- **The soak reaches its findings on a loaded box.** Record `uptime`'s load average beside any
  number taken from it, as the measurement above does.

## Done when

The soak is re-run after T20.22 and T20.23 land, with the room-count line quoted before and
after, and a written decision on `MAX_ROOMS` and on the auto-start window.

```sh
cargo run -p game-server --example loadgen -- --clients 48 --seconds 60 && ./scripts/check.sh
```
