# T21.19 — Dropped crates blink on the minimap (v9)

Milestone: M21 · Depends on: nothing · Size: ~150 lines, client only

Asked for by the coordinator: *"Dropped crates have a beacon. They appear as a
small red dot on the minimap for half a second, every 3 seconds. Will help
players find them and fight for them."*

## What already exists

- **The pulse.** `render/itemSprites-math.ts::beaconPulse` already drives the
  in-world glow on a crate. Whatever the minimap does should read from the same
  clock, or the dot and the glow will drift out of phase and look like two
  unrelated things.
- **Knowing it is a crate.** A world item carries `source === 'Crate'`
  (`isFallingCrate` uses it), so no new wire field is needed.
- **What the minimap does not have.** It draws players and geometry and has
  **never drawn items at all** — `minimap.ts` has no reference to them. That is
  the new part.

## Deliverable

A small red dot at each dropped crate's position on the minimap, visible for
**0.5 s out of every 3 s**.

## Decide, and say which at the code

The brief's timing means a player glancing at the minimap **misses the dot about
five times in six**. That may be exactly right — it makes crates findable without
turning the minimap into a permanent treasure map, and it rewards watching. It
may also be frustrating, given the stated goal is *helping* players find them.

**Default to the brief** — 0.5 s in 3 s — and put the tunables in `constants.rs`
so the other reading is one number away. Do not quietly "improve" it to a longer
blink; if it feels wrong in play that is the coordinator's call, and a constant
they can move is the way to hand it to them.

## Watch for

- **Do the crates all blink together?** A shared clock makes them pulse in unison,
  which reads as a UI element. A per-crate phase makes them look like independent
  beacons. Pick one deliberately — `birds.ts` and `animals.ts` both solved the
  same "a row of them must not be a chorus line" problem with a per-id phase.
- **A crate nobody has seen yet.** Does the dot appear for crates outside a
  player's view? The whole point is finding them, so presumably yes — but say so
  at the code, because it is the difference between a hint and a wallhack, and
  `docs/32` §5 deliberately withholds buried items for exactly that reason.
- **Dropped versus falling.** A crate under its parachute is not yet a pickup.
  Decide whether it blinks on the way down.
- The minimap is DOM and screen-space like every other overlay (§A35).

## Tests

- A dropped crate produces a dot; **no crate, no dot** — the control.
- The duty cycle is what the constants say: sampled across one full period, the
  dot is present for the on-window and absent for the rest. A test that only
  samples during the on-window cannot tell a blink from a permanent marker.
- The dot's position tracks the crate's, in minimap space.
- It goes when the crate is picked up.
- **Rendered pixels** (`docs/72` §C2): the dot is actually drawn, with a control
  region elsewhere on the minimap and a control frame during the off-window.
  Counting a `Graphics` call would pass against a dot drawn off-screen.

## Done when

```sh
npm --prefix client test -- --run && ./scripts/check.sh
```

## Resolution (builder, 2026-09-14)

**Decided, defaulting to the brief, and said at the code** (`MINIMAP_CRATE_PERIOD`'s doc, `beaconCrates`):

- **0.5 s in 3 s** — `MINIMAP_CRATE_ON` 0.5 / `MINIMAP_CRATE_PERIOD` 3.0, the brief, not "improved". Two
  numbers are the whole decision if it feels wrong in play.
- **Every crate blinks together, on the round clock** — one ping, and every client in step with the server.
  Not `beaconPulse`: a private 1 Hz sine on its own clock, the wrong shape for an on/off blink.
- **Landed crates only** — one still under its parachute is not a pickup yet.
- **Regardless of field of view** — the coordinator's ruling, recorded as a deliberate exception to §A6 for
  crates and nothing else. Buried items are not world items, so `docs/32` §5 is untouched.
- Red `0xff2a2a`, 3 cells — redder and darker than a remote player's `#ff5a5a`, and it blinks.
- The sandbox has no world items, so it passes no crates; that is nothing to beacon, not a divergence.

**Wiring.** `Minimap.update` takes `crates` and `roundTime` (required, not defaulted — the minimap drew no
items for the whole game), draws the dots under the local player's, and `stats()` reports `crates`,
`crateLit` and `crateDrawn`. `GameScene` passes `beaconCrates(mirror.items)`. Constants in all three places.

**Tests.** Unit, `minimap-beacon.test.ts`: lit for exactly ON/PERIOD of a whole sampled period, the
off-window as a control, periodic, a nonsense clock dark; `beaconCrates` beacons a landed crate, not a
falling one or a non-crate, nothing from nothing, and stops once the crate leaves the mirror. Browser,
`minimap-crates.mjs`, networked (seed 31337, no bots, no weather), reading **the minimap canvas's own
pixels**: no crate → no beacon pixel; a landed crate lit at its minimap cell and off otherwise — 87 samples
over 1.5 periods, share 0.21 against 0.167 — pixels agreeing with `stats()` in 87/87, and the far quarter of
the minimap never lit.

**Falsified**: an always-lit beacon reds "a marker, not a blink" (and the duty cycle, 100%); a scene passing
no crates reds "never showed" (0%). A first falsification run was **void** — a parity test rebuilt the wasm
under the running page (`reading 'debug'`) — and was re-run on a quiet tree. `constants-parity` also caught
the check reading `CRATE_INTERVAL`, which is not exported; the read is gone.

**Not covered in a browser**: a pickup removing the dot (asserted at unit level through the mirror).
