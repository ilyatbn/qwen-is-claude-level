# T21.12 — The teleport pad gets a gate, not a ring on the floor (v9)

Milestone: M21 · Depends on: nothing · Size: ~200 lines, **client and assets only**

**Reported from play by the coordinator**: *"make the teleport have a visible
sprite instead of a random ring on the floor. Same as the gun platform, it should
have a couple pixels of ground that you cannot destroy under it (i dont care if it
floats just make it look like it has some ground). pic is
`tasks/M21/assets/gate.png` — you should obviously remove the background and maybe
add a blue effect filling the ring when standing on the platform."*

## What is already true, and must not be re-implemented

**The indestructible ground exists.** `map/meta.rs::TeleportPad::rect` is `PAD_H`
rows of rock below the feet line across `PAD_W` columns, and
`map/carve.rs::carve_circle` subtracts every pad rect from every row it clears.
The doc comment on `TeleportPad` sets out the proof that a pad standable at
generation stays standable however much of the map is destroyed. **Verify it on
screen and leave it alone** — this task is the picture, not the rock.

If the verification shows the rock is *not* visible under a pad, that is a
finding worth reporting, and it changes this task. Check before assuming either
way.

## The art

`tasks/M21/assets/gate.png`, 545×602, a stone ring with runes on a brick base.

**It is fully opaque with a white background** — measured: 328 090 opaque pixels,
zero transparent, zero partial. So "remove the background" is real work, not a
copy:

* **Flood-fill from the borders**, do not threshold on whiteness. The stone has
  white highlights and a global "white → transparent" pass will punch holes
  through it.
* **The ring's interior is also white and must also go**, so the world shows
  through the portal. That is a second flood fill, from the centre. It is also
  where the blue effect goes.
* **Then downscale.** The game is `pixelArt: true` with NEAREST filtering, so
  drawing above native size only blurs. Size it from `PAD_W` so the sprite tracks
  the simulation rather than a literal: the brick base should read as sitting on
  the pad's protected rock.

**Write it as a build script**, `scripts/build-gate-sprite.mjs`, not as a
hand-edited PNG. Every other derived asset here is generated from a committed
source by a committed script — `build-atlas.mjs`, `build-object-masks.mjs`,
`build-cloud-atlas.mjs` — and a hand-edited binary is the one thing nobody can
review or redo.

Register the output in `assets/manifest.json` under `images[]`. **`build-atlas.mjs`
preserves `images`** (it merges rather than replaces — the comment there records
the bug that taught it), so this is safe. `scripts/verify-assets.mjs` checks every
manifest path exists, which is the gate that will notice a missing file.

## The blue effect

Replace the yellow charge arc with the portal filling blue as the charge runs
`0 → 1`. It must still be **driven by the snapshot byte**, never by a timer this
file runs.

`render/pads.ts`'s header explains why, and it is the whole design of that file:
the client could count the charge itself and would be wrong the moment the player
is unarmed, on cooldown, or a pixel off the pad — three rules that live in
`world::teleport`. **A byte on the wire is cheaper than three guards that drift.**

## Watch for

* **`PadLayer.count` and `PadLayer.ids` are asserted by the gate.**
  `scripts/checks/teleport.mjs` counts pads drawn against the pads the server
  sent. Keep both, whatever the entries hold.
* **Depth.** Pads draw at `DEPTH.decorations - 1`, deliberately: you stand *on* a
  pad so the player must draw over it, and sharing `DEPTH.decorations` would let a
  bush sort in front depending on creation order. A gate is a tall arch and you
  stand *inside* it — decide whether it still belongs behind the actors, and say
  which at the code. Behind is the safe answer; a player hidden by scenery is a
  worse bug than an arch that does not overlap them.
* **`padUnderfoot` is cosmetic only** and its header says so. It picks which pad
  gets the effect, never whether a teleport happens. Do not let the new art tempt
  anything gameplay-shaped into that function.
* **Every lookup falls back** (`docs/50` §8): the game must still boot and still
  show a usable pad indicator with no image on disk. Keep the drawn ring as the
  fallback rather than deleting it.
* `build()` is idempotent — calling it twice must not stack two gates per pad.
  It is tested; keep it true.

## Tests

* The build script, given the committed source, produces a sprite whose border
  pixels are transparent and whose stone is not. **Both halves** — an all-alpha
  image passes the first assertion alone.
* The ring interior is transparent, and the stone between interior and border is
  still opaque. That is the assertion that catches a flood fill that leaked.
* `PadLayer` still reports one entry per pad, and `build()` twice still reports
  one.
* With no image loaded, a pad still draws something. The fallback, asserted.
* **Rendered pixels** (`docs/72` §C2): the gate is visible at a pad, with a
  control region away from it. And the blue fill appears while charging, with a
  **control frame** taken at the same pad before the charge starts — a check that
  only photographs the charging frame cannot tell the effect from the sprite.
