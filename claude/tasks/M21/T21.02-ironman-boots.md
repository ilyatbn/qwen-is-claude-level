# T21.02 — Ironman boots (v9)

Milestone: M21 · Depends on: **T20.08** (passive pattern), **T20.04** and **T20.12** (anything
visible on a player) · Size: ~200 lines

Asked for: *"an item called 'ironman boots'. Having them in your inventory makes you run twice
as fast and jump three times higher. Make them red and yellow, and make sure it's visible on
the player."*

## Read first
- `crates/game-core/src/player/mod.rs:19-23,36-68` — `apply_input` **and its purity contract**
- `client/src/net/prediction.ts:71,106` — why that contract exists
- `crates/game-core/src/constants.rs` — `WALK_SPEED`, `JUMP_VELOCITY`
- `tasks/M20/T20.12-hats-and-sunglasses.md` — the accessory-rendering pattern and its traps

## ⚠ The hard part is not the multiplier — it is that movement is predicted

`apply_input` is called on the **server** (`world/mod.rs:1176`) *and* on the **client**, from
`prediction.ts`, dozens of times per frame for replay-reconciliation. Its doc comment says
purity is *the property that makes prediction correct*.

So a speed modifier that the server applies and the client does not **desyncs every step the
player takes** — and it will present as rubber-banding, not as a wrong speed. **The client
must know**, which means "carries boots" has to reach it.

**Read T20.07's analysis before choosing how.** It establishes that the live snapshot's flag
byte is *not* in the replay, so a derived bit costs nothing there — but that any **stored,
hashed** field is a `REPLAY_VERSION` bump. Prefer a bit derived at the encode site from the
inventory, exactly as T20.07 concludes for the flashlight.

## Deliverable

- A new `ItemId`, appended, in T20.08's passive family. Held, not used.
- **×2 run speed and ×3 jump height**, as two named constants in `constants.rs`. Note ×3
  *height* is **not** ×3 `JUMP_VELOCITY` — height goes as `v²/2g`, so ×3 height is
  **×√3 ≈ 1.732** velocity. Say which the constant means in its name and doc comment, or the
  next reader will assume the other.
- **The seam already exists — do not open a new one.** `apply_input` already takes a speed
  multiplier and threads it to `apply_horizontal`, where `let target = dir * WALK_SPEED *
  speed_multiplier`, and `movement.rs`'s comment states the design: *"The target speed is
  scaled by `speed_multiplier`, not the acceleration."* **Boots multiply into
  `PlayerState::speed_multiplier()`.**
- **Land T20.19 first.** The client currently passes a literal `1.0` where the server passes
  `speed_multiplier()`, so a hurt player already rubber-bands. Fixing that is the same fix
  boots need, and building boots on top of a broken seam would hide the defect behind a
  feature.
- **Visible on the player**, red and yellow.

## Notes

- **Interacts with T20.11 (fall damage).** ×3 jump height means a ×3 fall from the apex — with
  fall damage live, boots become a self-harm item unless that is deliberate. `v²/2g` from a
  ×√3 launch lands you at ×√3 the impact speed. **Decide and say so**; do not discover it.
- **`JUMP_HEIGHT` and `JUMP_REACH` are compile-time consts** in `traversal.rs`, derived from
  `JUMP_VELOCITY`, `WALK_SPEED` and `GRAVITY` — and they drive the **map generator's
  traversability check**. Boots let a player exceed the reachability model the map was built
  against. Probably fine (more mobility, not less), but **say so** rather than leaving it
  unstated. `traversal.rs`'s `"220 px is beyond jump reach {JUMP_REACH}"` assertion is the one
  that notices.
- **Interacts with T21.03 (unicorn wings).** Wings forbid jumping. Holding both is a state
  somebody will reach. Define it or refuse it.
- The visible half inherits **all** of T20.12's traps — `PlayerView` is rebuilt mid-round with
  `private readonly` appearance fields and no setter, so a boot drawn once vanishes on the
  next rebuild. Do not solve that twice; use whatever T20.04/T20.12 landed.
- **Red and yellow is not enough on its own.** `tombstoneTextures.ts:11-14`: variants must
  differ in **silhouette**, not palette — at `PLAYER_W = 16` a boot is a few pixels.

## Tests

- Speed and jump are multiplied, pinned to the constants, with a no-boots control.
- **The prediction test is the one that matters:** a client predicting a booted player's
  movement agrees with the server's. Without it this ships as rubber-banding.
- **Rendered pixels** with a control region and a control frame — a player with boots differs
  from one without, in the same frame.
- The boots survive a `PlayerView` rebuild.

## Tests at risk

- `movement.rs::the_speed_multiplier_caps_the_target_not_the_acceleration` — pins the exact
  semantics boots will ride on.
- `traversal.rs`'s jump-reach assertion, if the jump constant moves.
