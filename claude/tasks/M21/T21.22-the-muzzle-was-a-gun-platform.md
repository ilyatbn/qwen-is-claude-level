# T21.22 — "10 rockets left the muzzle": it was a gun platform (v9)

Milestone: M21 · Depends on: T21.11B/C, T21.14 · Size: small (two debug fields
and one browser check)

**Booked from T21.16's gate**, which recorded: *"`two-clients` reds
intermittently on a pre-existing gun-platform flake (4 pulls, 10 rockets =
2 bazooka + 2 barrages of 4)."* It failed inside a full gate and passed
standalone.

## The failure, and why the message was wrong

`scripts/e2e-two-clients.mjs` fires `BAZOOKA_AMMO` (4) times and requires
`observed.ownProjectileSpawns` to rise by exactly 4:

    FAIL: fired 4 but only 10 rockets left the muzzle

10 is not noise. A mounted player's trigger pull does not fire their bag — it
fires the platform (`world/mod.rs::fire_platform`), which spawns
`GUN_PLATFORM_BARRAGE` (4) projectiles **owned by that player**, so
`ownProjectileSpawns` counts every one. 2 ordinary pulls + 2 mounted pulls is
2 + 4 + 4 = 10, and there is no other decomposition: the only other multi-spawn
paths are the weather's, and those carry `owner = u8::MAX`
(`effects/meteor.rs`, `effects/toxic.rs`), so `ownProjectileSpawns` cannot see
them. **Measured, not reasoned**: a run with ana deliberately mounted reported
`fired 4 but only 16 rockets left the muzzle` — 4 x 4, exactly.

Ana never presses a movement key in this check, so any mount is free: the firing
loop sleeps `BAZOOKA_COOLDOWN * 1000 + 120` = 1020 ms between shots and
`GUN_PLATFORM_MOUNT_TIME` is 1.0 s, so **a player standing on a platform
re-mounts inside every gap, at any cadence**. There is no pacing that avoids it,
only a position.

**Standing still on a platform to mount it is the mechanic, not a bug.** The
defect is that nothing in the check could see it happening, so a true count
blamed the wrong mechanism.

## What was missing

The client already knows who is mounted — `MOVE_MOD.mounted` comes off the wire
and `GameScene.ts::occupiedPlatforms` lights the platform lamp with it — and
`render/platforms.ts::platformUnderfoot` already answers *which* platform a body
stands on. Neither reached `debug()`, so no browser check could ask.

## What this task did

1. **`debug().mount`**, in `GameScene.ts::exposeDebugHandle`'s `debug()`:
   `{ mounted, platformUnderfoot }`. **Two fields, kept separate**, because
   `occupiedPlatforms`'s own doc comment says they are two facts from two
   places: *whether* is the server's, off the wire, read from the mirror's
   snapshot rather than from prediction so a refused mount cannot appear;
   *which* is geometry. `platformUnderfoot` non-null with `mounted` false is a
   legal and common state — a mount takes a second of standing still, and an
   occupied platform refuses a second rider. Collapsing them would have made the
   step-off step below untestable: it has to watch the geometry change while the
   assertion watches the mount.
2. **`debug().platformPositions`**, beside them, for the reason `padPositions`,
   `mines` and `birdViews` exist — and specifically because the **presence
   control is impossible without it**: proving "she is not mounted" is not
   vacuous means deliberately walking her onto one, and a check cannot walk
   toward a position it cannot read. It also puts the diagnosis in the failure
   message instead of in the next session.
3. **`e2e-two-clients.mjs`**: before the firing loop, if a platform is underfoot
   she is walked clear — *and the field is re-read to prove she left*, never a
   keypress trusted. Then `mounted === false` is asserted immediately before
   **and** immediately after the loop.

The rocket count itself is **unchanged and exactly as strict**:
`spawnsAfter - spawnsBefore !== rockets`. No tolerance, no retry. A gate that
fails on a coin flip gates nothing; the cause gets named, not the threshold.

## Not by widening the clearance

`GUN_PLATFORM_SPAWN_CLEARANCE` was tried in T21.14 at the pad's value and
**starved the spawn sampler** (two platforms instead of three on Medium/seed 0).
It was reverted and that is recorded; do not go back there.

## What the measurement says, and the gap it leaves

**A platform was underfoot at spawn in 0 of 10 runs** (and 0 of 10 in an earlier
set), nearest platform 35–580 px. So T21.14's placement fix is holding at the
spawn point, and the residual route is **displacement during the loop** — her own
blast's knockback, or the crater undercutting the ground she stands on. She
presses nothing, so the after-loop assertion is the one that will catch it, and
its message says so.

**This is why the after-loop assertion exists and why it is not redundant.** An
assertion only before the loop is satisfied by a mount that happens in the first
1020 ms gap.

## Closing it: deny the mount, do not avoid the platform

Stepping off before the loop is necessary and **not sufficient**. Ana is
stationary for the whole loop, and anything that puts her back on a platform —
her own blast's knockback, or the crater undercutting her ground — hands the
mount rule a clear second. The gap is 1020 ms and `GUN_PLATFORM_MOUNT_TIME` is
1.0 s, so the mount wins that race at any cadence.

**The coordinator's premise was that movement resets the hold. It does not, and
that matters.** `mount::step`'s unmounted branch is the code that maintains
`held`:

    match under {
        Some(id) if grounded => { ...; st.held += dt; ... }
        _ => { st.target = None; st.held = 0.0; }
    }

Nothing there reads velocity or direction. A **walk** only resets the hold by
carrying her off the footprint; what resets it in place is failing that guard,
and `grounded` is half of it — one airborne tick drops into the `_` arm and
zeroes `held`. `step_mount` passes `body.grounded` straight through, so a jump
registers. **So the nudge is a hop, not a step sideways**, and her x is never
touched: the craters land where the blast-radius reasoning assumed, and the
cross-client terrain equality still compares two clients watching the same shots.

## Two defects the hop exposed, both measured before being fixed

1. **A press while airborne is the jetpack, not a jump.** `jetpack::update`'s
   post-jump branch engages on `jump_pressed && !body.grounded`, which bypasses
   `JETPACK_HOLD_DELAY` entirely. Pressing Space blind every cycle ran her tank
   from 5.0 to 3.2. A drained jetpack is a different player, so the hop presses
   **only from the ground**, and a `fuelFloor < fuelBefore` assertion pins it —
   falsified against the exact draining pattern, which reds with `5 ->
   3.215686274509804`.
2. **The firing gap was wall-clock; the cooldown is spent in the server's
   clock.** `sleep(BAZOOKA_COOLDOWN * 1000 + 120)` is 120 ms of headroom on 900,
   and the hop's extra load makes the server stall: one gap advanced the wall
   clock **1113 ms while `serverRoundTime` advanced 400 ms**. The next shot is
   then refused and the failure reads `fired 4 but only 3 rockets left the
   muzzle` — a true report about a refusal that says nothing about the muzzle.
   It cost **3 reds in 30 runs**. The gap now waits for the shot to be
   *accepted* (ana's own projectile spawns), anchors on the `serverRoundTime`
   read at that moment — conservative, because the spawn proves the server
   already stamped the shot — and then waits the cooldown in that same clock. A
   shot that is never accepted still fails loudly; this does not swallow a
   refusal.

   **Anchoring before the `fire()` call is wrong and was tried**: that sample is
   *earlier* than the round time the server stamps the shot at, so the wait is
   optimistic. It still red on the first smoke run.

## The deliberate control — the one that proves the hop, not the luck

The original failure has never been reproduced on demand, so a green run proves
nothing on its own. Staging it does. Ana is walked onto a platform, mounted (to
prove she is really there), then dismounted, so the loop **starts on a platform,
unmounted** — the exact state a blast produces. Identical staging, one variable:

| | after the loop | ana's own spawns for 4 pulls |
|---|---|---|
| **no hop** | `{"mounted":true,"platformUnderfoot":2}` | **13** = 1 + 4 + 4 + 4 |
| **hop** | `{"mounted":false,"platformUnderfoot":0}` | **4** |

The hop row is the important one: `platformUnderfoot` is still non-null, so she
was **standing on the platform the whole time and the mount never completed**.
That is denial, not avoidance.

## Falsification performed

- **The assertion reds.** Inverting both live comparisons in the script the check
  runs (`mounted !== false` -> `mounted !== true`) fails an ordinary run at both
  sites — so both are reached and evaluated every run, not skipped.
- **Presence control.** Ana walked deliberately onto platform #2 and mounted it;
  both assertions red with the new message naming the platform and her position,
  and the muzzle line read `fired 4 but only 16 rockets` — 4 barrages. Without
  this, "she is not mounted" would be satisfied by a game where nobody can mount.
- **The hop's own controls**: inverting nothing, the `gapsWithoutAir` and
  `fuelFloor` assertions both red against the configurations they name — the
  first when ana cannot leave the ground, the second against the exact blind
  press that drained the tank.
- **The step-off branch was exercised**, because on every map measured it is
  otherwise dead code: ana was walked onto platform #0, mounted, and the block
  recovered her (`stepped off: platformUnderfoot=null mounted=false`) and the
  check passed with 4 rockets for 4 pulls.
