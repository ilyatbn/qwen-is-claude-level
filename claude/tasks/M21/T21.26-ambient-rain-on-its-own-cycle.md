# T21.26 — Ambient rain, on its own cycle and not green (v9)

Milestone: M21 · Depends on: T21.25 (know whether the toxic rain works first)
· Size: medium — **touches `game-core` scheduling, so it is deterministic work**

## The ask

> *"the one that's just effects doesnt need to be green, it also doesnt need to
> show up as an event (or be part of the "bad" event cycle) - so move it into a
> different set of cycles which runs randomly like the day/night one or
> whatever."*

## ⚠ Read this before designing: there is no second rain to move

The request is shaped as *"move the cosmetic rain out of the hazard cycle"*, and
that is worth building — but the premise needs correcting, because **T20.05
deliberately joined the two rains** and this must not undo that fix.

There were once two independent rains: a 260-droplet screen-space sheet on a seed
of its own, and an unrelated set of real damaging drops. `TOXIC_DROPS_IN_FLIGHT`'s
doc comment records why that was wrong, in one sentence worth keeping:

> *"A player saw a downpour and was hit by a drizzle."*

So the emitter's density is now derived from the **live drop count**
(`toxicDensity(liveDrops, TOXIC_DROPS_IN_FLIGHT)` in `weather-math.ts`). The
cosmetic sheet no longer runs on its own at all — it is the toxic rain's picture.
**Decoupling it would restore exactly the bug T20.05 fixed.**

## So what to build instead

A **new ambient rain**: harmless, grey or blue rather than green, on its own
schedule, with no telegraph and no place in the hazard cycle.

- **Toxic rain keeps its sheet.** Its density stays derived from its real drops,
  and it stays green, telegraphed, and an event. Nothing about the hazard
  changes.
- **Ambient rain is a separate layer** that can be falling at the same time. Two
  rains on screen at once is fine and is what real weather does; what is not fine
  is one rain that sometimes hurts.
- **It must be visibly different from the toxic sheet** — colour is the cheap
  half, but consider density and speed too. The failure mode to design against is
  a player seeing rain and not knowing whether to run for cover. If they cannot
  tell at a glance, the feature is a hazard rather than a decoration.

## Where it lives

`effects/scheduler.rs` owns the hazard cycle (`EFFECT_INTERVAL_MIN`/`MAX`,
`EFFECT_TELEGRAPH`, and the per-kind durations). **Ambient rain does not belong in
it** — that scheduler's whole job is spacing out dangerous things with warning.

The model named in the request is the day/night cycle (`DAY_DURATION`,
`NIGHT_DURATION`, `CYCLE_LENGTH`), which runs on its own and is not an event.
Follow whichever of the two shapes fits, and say which you chose and why.

Either way it is **`game-core` and therefore seeded**: `ChaCha8Rng` only, on its
own sub-stream, never `thread_rng`. Two clients must agree on whether it is
raining, or the replay diverges.

## Tests

- Over many seeds, ambient rain **happens**, and its share of the round sits in a
  sensible band. A population claim needs more than one draw.
- **It never damages anyone.** With the presence control that makes that mean
  something: a toxic shower in the same test *does* (whatever T21.25 concludes).
  "No damage" is otherwise satisfied by a game that deals none.
- It is **not** telegraphed and does not appear in the hazard/event UI.
- **Rendered pixels**: the ambient sheet is visibly a different colour from the
  toxic sheet, with a control frame.
- Determinism: two clients on one seed agree on when it rains.

## Watch for

- `REPLAY_VERSION` — adding a draw to a seeded stream moves everything after it.
  Bump it, and say so in the journal.
- The existing `rainDrops` / `rainPool` debug fields mean *the toxic sheet*. A
  second rain reusing them is the field-that-means-two-things bug, and
  `toxic-rain-game.mjs` reads them.
