# T21.37 — Tinted skins on the Canvas renderer

Milestone: M21 · Depends on: T21.33 · Size: ~200 lines over `playerView.ts`, one helper, one check

## Finding (T21.33's audit)

`render/playerView.ts` colours the one tinted skin — **Recruit (red)**, id 5 in `assets/skins.json`,
`"tint": "0xff8a7a"` over skin 0's frames — with `Sprite.setTint`. Phaser's Canvas renderer has no
sprite tint, so on Canvas that skin draws **untinted: exactly the Recruit**. Two players who chose
different skins look identical. The owner's browser runs Canvas, and so does any player without
WebGL.

## Audit — every tint a Canvas player sees

`grep -rn "setTint\|setTintFill\|tintFill\|\.tint =" client/src`, 2026-09-15:

| Site | Object | Carries meaning? | Ruling |
|---|---|---|---|
| `playerView.ts` `PlayerView` constructor, `body.setTint(parseTint(skin.tint))` | player body Sprite: local, remote, sandbox, skins preview (all four `new PlayerView` callers) | **yes — skin identity** | **fix** |
| `parallax.ts` `ParallaxLayer.update`, ridge `TileSprite.setTint` | background ridges | colour of scenery | already fixed by T21.33 (`recolourRidge`) |
| `sky.ts` sun glow `Image.setTint` | cosmetic halo; the disc warms through `setFillStyle` | no | cosmetic, left (as T21.33 ruled) |

No `setTint` / `setTintFill` on graves, items, weapons, accessories, HUD icons or text. The
placeholder body (`placeholderTexture`) and item fallback boxes (`itemSprites.ts`) bake their colour
into a canvas or a `Rectangle` fill, both of which Canvas draws.

## Ruling (builder)

- **Bake, do not share `recolourRidge`.** That helper is `source-in`: it floods every opaque texel
  with one flat colour, which is right for a white silhouette and wrong for character art — the
  Recruit would become a salmon blob. A sprite tint is a **per-channel multiply** (WebGL:
  `texel * tint`), so the bake multiplies each texel's RGB by the tint and keeps its alpha.
- **Once per (atlas, tint)**, as a copy of the whole atlas under `<atlas>__tint_<rrggbb>` with every
  frame re-added at the same name and rectangle. The frames keep their names, so `framesFor` is
  unchanged; the animations for a baked skin reference the baked key. `animKey` is already per skin
  id, so skin 5's anims never collide with skin 0's.
- **WebGL keeps `setTint`**: the bake runs only when `hasWebGL` is false.
- **Check hook**: `__game.showSkins(ids | null)` in the sandbox builds extra `PlayerView`s beside the
  player, so a tinted and an untinted body are in one frame. Dev surface only.

## Resolution (builder, 2026-09-15)

**Fixed** in `render/playerView.ts` (constructor: `hasWebGL` false and a tint → frames and anims
from `canvasTint.ts::bakeTintedAtlas`; WebGL still `setTint`) and `render/canvasTint.ts` (per-texel
multiply on a copy of the atlas, every frame re-added by name, trim copied). One bake per
(atlas, tint), kept for the game. Sandbox hook `__game.showSkins`.

**Green:** `canvas-tinted-skin` — subject (Recruit red) 2581 body px, warmth R/(G+B) 1.017; control
(Recruit) 2572 px, 0.552 → **1.84x** (tint predicts 1.96x, bar 1.48x), drift 0. The screenshot shows a
red Recruit beside a plain one. `canvas-renderer` green. Vitest 922/922, typecheck clean. WebGL
`skins`, `sandbox` green.

**Falsified at the live site:** the bake call in `PlayerView`'s constructor replaced by
`this.skinDef.atlas` → subject warmth 0.551 vs control 0.552, **1.00x**, red "the tint is not reaching
the Canvas picture".

**Not covered.** The WebGL path of skin 5 has no pixel check (unchanged code, `setTint`). A trimmed
atlas is handled by `setTrim` but `chars.json` has no trimmed frame, so that branch has never run.
