# T21.21 — Rocks and trees float; seat them in the ground (v9)

Milestone: M21 · Depends on: nothing · Size: **large, and it moves the golden
table** — split it, see the end

**Reported from play by the coordinator**: *"The random objects (rocks, crystals,
trees etc) doesn't look seamless enough. Some of them are semi floating in the
air, others are placed fine but appear semi in the air, as if not part of the
map."*

Asked for, in their words:

1. *"These shapes don't have to be vertical. They can be placed in an angle on
   slopes. Server needs to calculate that in."*
2. *"Modify the map to add missing pixels of ground. A rock with the base of 40px
   cannot be on a flat surface of 28 pixels. Extend the ground to make it appear
   seamless."*

## What the code does today, and why it produces exactly this

`map/gen/objects.rs::seat` places an object at the **median** ground height under
its footprint, and `OBJECT_FOOTPRINT_SUPPORT` refuses it only if fewer than 60 %
of its columns sit within a band of that seat.

The constant's own doc comment says this is intended:

> *"roughly half its base is buried in the slope and half stands proud. Partial
> burial is correct and wanted: a boulder half-sunk in a hillside is what a
> boulder looks like."*

**The report says otherwise, and the report wins.** A median seat on a slope
means up to 40 % of the base may hang with nothing under it — which is precisely
"semi floating". The rule was reasoned about rather than looked at.

There is **no rotation at all**: `PlacedObject.flip` is a horizontal mirror, and
nothing else.

## ⚠ Three things that make this risky

1. **An object's silhouette *is* collision.** The mask is stamped at generation
   (§D1), so changing how an object sits changes the terrain players stand on and
   shoot through. This is not a render change.
2. **The golden table will move**, and that is expected here rather than a
   failure: `golden.rs::generated_masks_match_the_golden_table` pins terrain
   geometry. Regenerating it is the T9.04 / T15.02 procedure, including the
   999-seed sweep. **Do not nudge the golden file to match; regenerate it, and
   re-run the sweep.** A golden updated without the sweep is a golden that
   records a bug.
3. **The art has to follow the mask.** The client draws the sprite from an atlas;
   if the server tilts the stamp and the client draws it upright, the picture and
   the collision disagree — which is worse than floating. An angle must reach the
   client, which means the object section of `map_init` grows a field.

## Do part 2 first

**Extending the ground is the smaller, safer half** and may be most of the fix on
its own. It changes the mask under an object rather than the object, needs no
wire change, and needs no rotation maths. Ship it, look at it, and only then
decide how much rotation is still wanted.

Rotation is the part that grows the wire, resamples a 1-bit mask (jagged edges
are the hazard), and has to keep the sprite and the stamp in agreement.

## Watch for

- **Do not fill the ground upward into the object.** The point is contact under
  the base, not burying it. A fill that swallows the rock is the opposite defect.
- **Filling changes traversability.** `traversable_fraction` and the connectivity
  sweep both read the mask; adding ground can bridge a gap that generation
  deliberately left, or seal a cave. The sweep is the instrument.
- **`OBJECT_FOOTPRINT_SUPPORT` may become dead.** If the ground is extended to
  meet the base, the 60 % rule has less to refuse. Decide whether it stays as a
  backstop or goes — and if it goes, say so, rather than leaving a constant
  nothing reads.
- **Objects near the void.** A rock at the map edge must not grow ground out into
  the kill boundary.
- **Rotation and the anchor.** `assets/objects/manifest.json` gives each object an
  anchor at its base centre. Rotating about anything else swings the object off
  its seat.

## Tests

- **A seated object has ground under its whole base**, across many seeds and all
  scales. **Control**: the same measurement before the change fails — this is the
  bug, so it must be reproducible as a number before it is fixed.
- No object gains ground it does not need: total solid pixels added is bounded,
  and an object already on flat ground gains **none**.
- Traversability is not silently improved: the connectivity sweep still reports
  what it did, within its documented tolerance.
- The golden table is regenerated **with** the sweep re-run, and the journal says
  so.
- **Rendered pixels** (`docs/72` §C2): a rock on a slope, before and after, with a
  control region of plain terrain. This is a looks bug, so the assertion has to
  be a picture.
- For rotation, if it is done: the drawn sprite's angle equals the stamped mask's
  angle. Counting them separately is the only way to catch a client that ignores
  the field.

## Split

1. **T21.21A — extend the ground under a seated object.** Mask only, no wire, no
   rotation. Golden regenerated, sweep re-run.
2. **T21.21B — tilt objects to the slope.** Angle on the wire, sprite follows the
   stamp. Only if A leaves it still looking wrong.

## Resolution (builder, 2026-09-14) — T21.21A landed; B not needed yet

**Measured first**, on the finished maps of six seeds at three scales, before any change. Counting every
column a silhouette covers said 52.5 % hang — but that counts a boulder's flanks curving up away from the
ground. Counting the **base** (the silhouette's bottom row) is the report's claim: **21.9 % of base columns
touched nothing, in 347 of 438 objects; gap median 6 px, 90th percentile 224, tail past 1000.** A hover,
then cliffs.

**A — the ground meets the base.** `fill_under` (after `stamp`, in `stamp_objects`) fills straight down from
each base column to the first solid pixel, if that is within `OBJECT_GROUND_FILL_DEPTH` (1.0) of the
object's height; deeper is a cliff or cave mouth, and growing rock into it would be a stalk. Below the
silhouette only, never into the walls, charged to the pixel budget. `OBJECT_FOOTPRINT_SUPPORT` **stays**, as
the backstop that refuses a rock spanning a chasm; its doc and `seat`'s no longer claim a median seat or call
half-hanging "correct and wanted".

**Tests.** `a_seated_object_meets_the_ground_under_its_whole_base`: without the fill (the control) 3603 base
columns hover; with it **0**; 348 perched past the reach either way. `an_object_on_flat_ground_gains_nothing`
over every object in the table. `the_fill_stops_at_the_base_and_is_bounded`: nothing at or above the base
row changes, the gap closes exactly, and a gap past the reach is left alone. `every_placed_object_rests_on_
the_ground_under_it` and the other placement tests green; game-core 1089 passed.

**Sweep, before → after** (999 maps, `--release --ignored`): fraction min 0.754 → 0.754, p05 0.873 → 0.872,
p50 0.958 → 0.958; caves reachable 82.0 % → 81.8 %; attempts [956, 41, 2] → [955, 42, 2]; no safe preset,
no failures. **Golden regenerated with it** (`GOLDEN_UPDATE=1`, all 24 cases moved, as they must) and green.

**B — tilting to the slope — not done**, per this file's own split ("only if A leaves it still looking wrong"):
it grows the wire and resamples a 1-bit mask, and A has not been looked at in play yet.

**Not covered**: no browser before/after — the fill happens at generation, so no runtime switch shows one map
both ways. The mask is asserted in Rust; that the renderer draws the mask is `terrain-render`'s.
