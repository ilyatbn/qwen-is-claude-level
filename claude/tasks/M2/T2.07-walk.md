## Deliverable

```rust
/// Apply horizontal acceleration for one tick. Does not move the body —
/// integration (T2.05) does that.
pub fn apply_horizontal(
    body: &mut Body,
    dir: f32,               // -1, 0, +1 from Input::move_dir
    speed_multiplier: f32,  // 1.0 for now; T4.12 supplies the real value
    dt: f32,
);
```

Rules, exactly as the doc's §3:

| State | Input | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Grounded | direction held | `approach(vel.x, dir * WALK_SPEED * mult, WALK_ACCEL * dt)` |
| Grounded | nothing | `approach(vel.x, 0, GROUND_FRICTION * dt)` |
| Airborne | direction held | `approach(vel.x, dir * WALK_SPEED * mult, WALK_ACCEL * AIR_ACCEL_FACTOR * dt)` |
| Airborne | nothing | `approach(vel.x, 0, AIR_DRAG * dt)` |

## Notes

Use `approach` from `math.rs` (T0.04) for all four cases. Do not hand-roll clamped
acceleration here — that helper exists precisely so these four rules cannot drift
apart.

Note that the **target speed** is scaled by `speed_multiplier`, not the
acceleration. Scaling acceleration instead makes a wounded player feel sluggish to
control rather than simply slower, which is not what
`docs/21-player-stats.md` §3 specifies.

Air drag is deliberately much weaker than ground friction (120 vs 1600): you keep
most of your momentum in the air, which is what makes the jump-then-steer feel in
`docs/20-player-movement.md` §4 work.

This function does not read the map and does not move anything. Keeping
acceleration and integration separate is what lets the jetpack (T2.10) layer its own
thrust on top without duplicating any of this.

## Tests

- Grounded, holding right from rest: `vel.x` increases and converges to exactly
  `WALK_SPEED` — assert it lands on the value, not merely near it.
- Grounded, no input, from `vel.x = WALK_SPEED`: decelerates to exactly 0 and stays
  there (does not oscillate around zero).
- Time to reach full speed from rest is `WALK_SPEED / WALK_ACCEL` seconds, within
  one tick.
- Airborne acceleration is exactly `AIR_ACCEL_FACTOR` times the grounded rate —
  measure ticks to reach half speed in each state and compare.
- Airborne, no input: decelerates, but far more slowly than grounded (assert the
  ratio matches `AIR_DRAG / GROUND_FRICTION`).
- Reversing direction at full speed crosses zero and reaches full speed the other
  way; count the ticks and assert it matches the acceleration.
- `speed_multiplier = 0.75` caps `vel.x` at `0.75 * WALK_SPEED`.
- `speed_multiplier = 0` brings the body to a stop.
- `dir = 0` never accelerates in either direction.
- `apply_horizontal` never touches `vel.y` or `pos`.
