## Deliverable

The single entry point the server tick loop and the client predictor both call.

```rust
/// Everything a player's own input does in one tick. Pure: same inputs from the
/// same state produce byte-identical results.
pub fn apply_input(
    map: &Map,
    body: &mut Body,
    jump: &mut JumpState,
    jet: &mut JetpackState,
    input: &Input,
    prev: &Input,
    speed_multiplier: f32,
    dt: f32,
);
```

Order, and it is part of the contract:

1. `edges(input, prev)`
2. `apply_horizontal(body, input.move_dir(), speed_multiplier, dt)`
3. `let jumped = try_jump(body, jump, edges.jump_pressed, input.move_dir())`
4. `jetpack::update(jet, body, input.held(JUMP), edges.jump_pressed, jumped, dt)`
5. if `jet.active` → `jetpack::apply_thrust(body, input, dt)`
6. `integrate(map, body, jetpack::gravity_scale(jet), dt)`

## Notes

Step 3 before step 4 is what implements the Space disambiguation: the jump consumes
a grounded press, and the jetpack only sees what is left.

Step 6 last: all forces are accumulated into velocity first, then the body moves
once. Moving between force applications would make the result depend on the order
of the forces, which is exactly the kind of thing that diverges between server and
client under reordering.

**No randomness, no time source, no logging, no allocation.** This function is
called dozens of times per frame by the client during reconciliation replay
(`docs/42-netcode-prediction.md` §2), and its purity is the property that makes
prediction correct. If anything in the call chain is impure, prediction silently
drifts and the bug surfaces as rubber-banding weeks later.

## Tests

`crates/game-core/tests/movement_scenarios.rs`, against small hand-built masks —
a flat floor, a 6 px step, a 20 px wall, a 45° ramp, a 1 px spike:

**Determinism** (the foundation):
- Same start state + same 1000-input sequence → byte-identical final state, run 100
  times.
- Replaying inputs 500..1000 from the state at tick 500 reaches the same final state
  as running 0..1000 straight through. This is the reconciliation identity from
  `docs/42-netcode-prediction.md` §9, and if it fails, netcode cannot work.

**No tunnelling** — run for every axis and both directions:
- A body at 10× `MAX_FALL_SPEED` is stopped by a 1 px wall.
- A body at 10× `WALK_SPEED` horizontally is stopped by a 1 px wall.

**Scenarios** (the doc's §8 list):
- Standing on flat ground: position bit-identical after 600 ticks.
- Walks up a 6 px step; blocked by a 7 px step.
- Blocked by a 20 px wall, `vel.x` zeroed.
- Walking down a 30° slope keeps `grounded` true every tick.
- Jump apex within 2 px of the analytic value.
- Standing jump lands within 2 px of its origin.
- Running jump travels further than a standing jump.
- Holding the opposite direction mid-air reverses horizontal velocity within
  `WALK_SPEED / (WALK_ACCEL * AIR_ACCEL_FACTOR)` seconds.
- Coyote time works at 5 ticks, fails at 12.
- 5.0 s of held thrust drains fuel to 0; 10.0 s idle refills to 5.0.
- Jetpack does not engage from a grounded press until 0.18 s have passed.
- A player cannot climb a sheer wall by holding a direction while airborne.
